# Zero-velocity detection and motion-mode gating for a moving metro

Our evaluation isolated one dominant failure: the production EKF's variance-threshold zero-velocity update (ZUPT), a detector inherited from pedestrian foot-mounted navigation, misfires on the vibration dips of a train at constant cruise, zeroes the velocity state, and because tangential acceleration during cruise is ~0, the filter cannot rebuild speed and position stalls. Disabling ZUPT alone dropped in-tunnel error from 3108 m to 184 m. The literature of the last decade says, almost unanimously, that this is the expected outcome of porting a foot-stop detector to a vehicle, and it points to two concrete remedies: gate the ZUPT on an explicit motion state with hysteresis and a minimum dwell duration, and (the direction the field has actually moved) replace binary zero-velocity detection with a learned velocity/displacement regressor whose uncertainty does the gating continuously. Given WakePoint's generous on-device budget, the strongest recommendation is to fuse a learned along-route velocity measurement into the EKF and demote ZUPT to a station-dwell-only update; the minimal immediately-shippable fix is to gate the existing ZUPT on a three-state motion classifier. The evidence for each step follows.

## Why a pedestrian ZUPT breaks on a train

The zero-velocity update was popularised for shoe-mounted pedestrian inertial navigation, where the foot is genuinely stationary for a fraction of every stride and the resulting pseudo-measurement bounds the otherwise cubic growth of double-integration drift. The entire method rests on an assumption that a metro violates: that "not moving" is a frequent, short, detectable event. Wahlström and Skog's review of the field is explicit that even within pedestrian navigation the optimal detector threshold depends on gait speed and there is no fixed threshold robust across motion types ([Wahlström & Skog 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09208)). A train breaks the assumption in the opposite direction from fast running: the vehicle spends minutes in a near-constant-velocity regime whose specific-force signature, a low-variance accelerometer with superimposed bogie/rail vibration, is exactly what a variance-threshold detector was designed to read as "stationary." This is not a tuning accident but a structural mismatch, and it is the same mechanism Skog, Hendeby and Kok diagnose even in the foot-mounted case: a detection threshold set high enough to catch stances also fires while the sensor is moving, "cutting away" part of each motion segment and injecting an along-track bias ([Skog, Hendeby & Kok 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09363)). In our 1D-along-route filter the injected error is not a trimmed step but a fully zeroed cruise velocity that cannot recover.

Two design errors in the production filter compound the mismatch, and both are named in this literature. First, a ZUPT observes velocity, not position; tightening the position covariance after every ZUPT, as the production filter does, is unsupported by the measurement model and is a direct cause of the NEES≈4633 overconfidence we measured. Second, the filter caps position sigma at 200 m to keep 2D station association tractable, which forces the covariance to lie about its own confidence. The consistent-filtering literature treats these as the same disease: Wahlström's Bayesian detector reframes the threshold as a hypothesis test with an explicit, time-varying prior and an explicit cost of a missed detection versus a false alarm, so that the decision adapts to how long it has been since the last genuine stop rather than to a hand-tuned constant ([Wahlström et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07929)). The lesson for us is that the ZUPT decision must carry a cost model, and the covariance update must respect what the pseudo-measurement actually observes.

## Learned and adaptive zero-velocity detection

The 2017–2020 wave of work replaced the fixed threshold with a learned or motion-conditioned decision, and it is the most directly transferable body of results for a "keep ZUPT but gate it better" path. Wagstaff and Kelly first showed that a small LSTM reading raw IMU windows detects stationarity robustly across walking, running, stair-climbing, crawling and ladder-climbing, cutting 3D position error by over 34% relative to fixed-threshold detectors and, critically for a product, removing the per-motion threshold tuning entirely ([Wagstaff & Kelly 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/IPIN.2018.8533770)). Their follow-up paired that LSTM classifier with an alternative that augments a classical detector with a motion classifier that adaptively updates the threshold, and added a data-augmentation trick to port the LSTM to a new IMU without recollecting training data ([Wagstaff, Peretroukhin & Kelly 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSEN.2019.2944412)). The conceptual root of both is their earlier result that an SVM classifying six motion types in real time, each with its own F-score-optimal ZUPT parameters, reduces INS error during mixed motion ([Wagstaff, Peretroukhin & Kelly 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/IPIN.2017.8115947)). The through-line, classify the motion then condition the zero-velocity decision on that class, is exactly the gating our filter lacks, and all of it is released as the open-source PyShoe INS.

The most complete statement of "gate the update on motion state" is Skog, Hendeby and Kok's jump-Markov filter bank, which describes motion mode and navigation state in a single jump-Markov model and runs a bank of Kalman filters for joint inference of state and mode, learning the mode-transition probabilities from data ([Skog, Hendeby & Kok 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09363)). One of their two worked systems does precisely what we need in miniature: it adaptively selects the covariance used in the zero-velocity update according to the inferred mode, rather than applying a fixed ZUPT whenever a threshold trips. Transposed to a metro, the modes become {dwell-at-platform, cruise, accelerate/decelerate}, the transition probabilities encode that dwells are reached only through deceleration and are sustained, and the ZUPT is emitted with finite (not certainty-collapsing) covariance only in the dwell mode.

## Odometry that never zeroes velocity

The direction the field has actually taken since 2019 is to abandon binary zero-velocity detection and instead regress motion directly from the IMU, so that "stopped" is simply a near-zero displacement output with its own covariance and no detector is needed. RIDI opened this line by regressing a velocity vector from the history of linear acceleration and angular velocity and using it to correct low-frequency accelerometer bias before integration ([Yan, Shan & Furukawa 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09004)). RoNIN generalised it, regressing velocity in a fixed, orientation-agnostic frame and releasing the 42.7-hour, 100-subject benchmark that still anchors the field ([Yan, Herath & Furukawa 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12853)). TLIO is the template most relevant to us because it is a filter, not an end-to-end network: it regresses 3D displacement and its uncertainty from IMU windows and tightly fuses that as the measurement update of a stochastic-cloning EKF solving for pose, velocity and biases, outperforming raw velocity integration ([Liu et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2020.3007421)). NILoc then showed the velocity sequence can be mapped all the way to an absolute location with a transformer when a location prior exists ([Herath et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15851)), conceptually close to our known-route setting, where the "map" is a 1D arc-length polyline.

The architectural refinements since 2021 matter for on-device deployment and for the stop-vs-cruise problem specifically. CTIN and RIOT brought contextual and recursive transformers to velocity/displacement regression with multi-task uncertainty ([Rao et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02143); [Brotchie et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01641)). EqNIO addressed the single biggest generalisation weakness for a phone in an unknown pocket orientation: it canonicalises IMU data into a learnable gravity-aligned frame so the displacement prior need not memorise every mounting and heading, and it wraps around TLIO and RoNIN rather than replacing them, with code released ([Jayanth et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06321)). IONext reports that a carefully designed CNN backbone still beats transformer baselines on six public datasets, which is relevant when the target is a phone CPU ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17089)). The most on-point single paper for our dominant bug is GNIO (2026): its "Gated Prediction Head" decomposes displacement into magnitude and direction and acts as a soft, differentiable zero-velocity update that suppresses noise during stationarity and scales up during motion, explicitly targeting the "micro-drift during stationarity" and "frequent stops" cases and reporting a 60% trajectory-error reduction on OxIOD ([Feng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15281)). This is, in effect, the principled replacement for the binary ZUPT we are fighting. MosaicIMU and KISS-IMU round out the frontier on the two axes we care about operationally: cross-platform generalisation via a mixture-of-experts fused with a history-aware EKF ([Zou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09355)), and eliminating the ground-truth-trajectory training dependency through self-supervision with uncertainty-aware inference ([Choi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06205)).

## Knowing you are stopped, not merely shaken

Distinguishing a ~20 s platform dwell from a sub-second cruise vibration dip is a classification-plus-duration problem, and two literatures inform it. The transport-mode-recognition field has established that smartphone IMU data separates the relevant coarse states: its canonical benchmark, the Sussex-Huawei Locomotion dataset, labels eight modes that include still, train and subway as distinct classes, direct evidence that "stationary," "on a cruising train," and "walking" are separable from inertial signatures alone. Moreau et al. show this need not be heavy, with a deliberately small CNN matching state-of-the-art transport-mode accuracy on GeoLife and SHL at 10–1000× fewer parameters, an important existence proof for an always-on phone classifier ([Moreau et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2021.3110949)). Tang and Cheng reach 95.1% over eight modes using only accelerometer, gyroscope and magnetometer ([Tang & Cheng 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11087)), and Siargkas et al. fuse low-rate acceleration with location under an attention-based multiple-instance model specifically to hold accuracy while cutting battery draw ([Siargkas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2024.3387834)), relevant because a wake-alarm app runs the classifier continuously. The emerging foundation-model framing (LLM-style zero-shot HAR) is worth tracking but is not yet a deployment answer for a latency- and power-bound gate ([Ji et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02727)).

The crucial gating principle, though, is duration and spatial consistency, not instantaneous class. A dwell is a sustained low-motion window of roughly 15–25 s that coincides with a predicted station arc-position; a vibration dip is transient and occurs at an arbitrary arc-position. The train-localization literature supplies both the mechanism and the strongest analog to our own problem. Löffler and Bengtsson tackle almost exactly our setting, a train under GNSS outages with IMU plus a known track, using a minimalist particle filter over a discrete track-map look-up table, holding absolute error below 10 m through GNSS outages of up to 30 s and noting that accuracy holds specifically on curves and curvy lines ([Löffler & Bengtsson 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02339)); this validates both our arc-length-map approach and our earlier curvature finding that turns carry information. Winter et al. earlier showed that track-geometry constraints improve cross-track accuracy over a standard Kalman filter and validated against OpenStreetMap geometry ([Winter, Willert & Adamy 2019](https://doi.org/10.25534/tuprints-00011296)). For the metro case specifically, Ye et al.'s MLoc demonstrates that accelerometer, magnetometer and barometer readings form reproducible per-segment patterns along a metro line, achieving 93% localization accuracy over three stations and 98% over five with no infrastructure ([Ye et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531)), evidence that station-to-station segments and stops are inertially distinctive and that a barometer is a cheap dwell/segment cue. Finally, when the filter is in cruise and must not apply ZUPT, the vehicle-navigation literature shows the velocity state can instead be held by a learned forward-speed pseudo-measurement: OdoNet regresses a pseudo-odometer speed from a single IMU and cuts positioning error ~68% versus a non-holonomic-constraint-only baseline ([Tang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03091)), Freydin and Or regress car speed from a six-axis IMU as an aided-dead-reckoning pseudo-measurement ([Freydin & Or 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/LSENS.2022.3201731)), and Klein's PiDR shows a physics-informed residual keeps pure-inertial dead reckoning stable under sparse supervision on resource-constrained platforms ([Sahoo & Klein 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03040)).

## Recommendation for the WakePoint 1D EKF

The ranked plan below is ordered by expected impact given the generous on-device budget, with the cheapest robust fix called out for immediate shipping.

1. **Replace the binary variance-threshold ZUPT with motion-state gating (ship this first).** Run a lightweight three-state classifier {dwell, cruise, accel/decel} on the IMU (a small CNN or SVM in the Moreau/Wagstaff mould is enough) and permit a zero-velocity pseudo-measurement only in the dwell state. Add hysteresis and a minimum-dwell-duration requirement (~5–10 s of sustained low motion before the state may enter `dwell`, plus a fixed exit debounce) so a sub-second cruise vibration dip can never trigger a ZUPT. This is the Wagstaff motion-classification-conditioned ZUPT and the Skog–Hendeby–Kok filter-bank pattern transposed to a vehicle, and it directly removes the mechanism that stalled our filter.
2. **During cruise, maintain velocity with a learned forward-speed pseudo-measurement instead of ever zeroing it.** Regress along-route speed from the IMU (OdoNet / Freydin-style) and fuse it as a velocity update projected onto the known route tangent. This is what lets the velocity state rebuild, the property whose absence made a single spurious ZUPT unrecoverable in cruise.
3. **Best long-term: fuse a learned displacement+covariance regressor as the EKF update, à la TLIO/GNIO, and let its uncertainty replace the detector.** A GNIO-style gated head is a soft, differentiable ZUPT: at a genuine stop it emits ~0 displacement with tight covariance; in cruise it scales the displacement up, with no binary trigger and no unrecoverable zeroing. Project the regressed displacement onto the 1D route arc; wrap the network in EqNIO-style gravity-aligned canonicalisation so it tolerates arbitrary phone pocket/hand orientation (our known weak point). The on-device budget makes a compact CNN backbone (IONext-class) feasible.
4. **Detect the metro dwell by duration and spatial consistency, not variance alone.** Declare a stop only when a sustained (~15–25 s) low-motion window co-occurs with proximity to the next expected station arc-position from the route polyline; optionally confirm with the barometer/segment pattern (MLoc) or the published schedule. A transient dip fails both the duration and the spatial test. Löffler's track-map particle filter is the proof this holds through 30 s outages on curved lines.
5. **Fix the covariance model regardless of which ZUPT path is chosen.** Stop tightening the position covariance on a ZUPT: the pseudo-measurement observes velocity, so position variance may change only through the state cross-correlation, never by direct shrinkage. Remove the 200 m sigma cap and make station association robust to a large, honest sigma by gating candidates on 1D route ordering / arc-distance rather than a 2D spatial candidate set; this dissolves the self-defeating loop where the filter shrank sigma to keep 2D association tractable and thereby manufactured its own NEES≈4633 overconfidence. Add NEES/NIS consistency monitoring to the simulator as an acceptance gate for every change above.

## Reference software and datasets

- **PyShoe** — https://github.com/utiasSTARS/pyshoe — Python zero-velocity-aided error-state EKF with four classical detectors plus the learned SVM/LSTM detectors, and mixed-motion datasets; the reference implementation for the gating path (recommendations 1–2).
- **OpenShoe** — https://github.com/hcarlsso/ZUPT-aided-INS (MATLAB framework; project at openshoe.org) — the classical ZUPT-aided INS and detector-evaluation framework PyShoe builds on.
- **TLIO** — https://github.com/CathIAS/TLIO — displacement-regression + stochastic-cloning EKF; the template for recommendation 3.
- **RoNIN** — https://github.com/Sachini/ronin — velocity-regression networks (ResNet/LSTM/TCN) and the 42.7 h benchmark; NILoc project page: https://sachini.github.io/niloc.
- **EqNIO** — https://github.com/RoyinaJayanth/EqNIO — gravity-aligned subequivariant canonicalisation that wraps around TLIO/RoNIN for orientation robustness (ICLR 2025).
- **Sussex-Huawei Locomotion (SHL) dataset** — http://www.shl-dataset.org — smartphone multimodal benchmark labelling eight modes including distinct still, train and subway classes; the training resource for the motion-state gate (recommendation 1), with an annual UbiComp/ISWC challenge.
