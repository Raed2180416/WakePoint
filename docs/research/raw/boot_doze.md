# Surviving reboot and Doze on Android 14/15/16 for GeoWake (Android-first India transit wake-alarm)

## Summary
On Android 14/15/16 you CAN start a `location` foregroundServiceType service from a BOOT_COMPLETED receiver — location is NOT on the list of FGS types banned from BOOT_COMPLETED (that list is dataSync/camera/mediaPlayback/mediaProjection/phoneCall on 15, plus microphone on 14). The real blocker is the while-in-use rule: because BOOT_COMPLETED runs while the app is "in the background," a location FGS started there throws ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException UNLESS ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION is already granted. So GeoWake's reboot-restart path is only viable if the user has granted "Allow all the time" background location (settings-only on Android 11+, requested separately after foreground is granted, and gated by a Google Play Permissions Declaration review). For never-miss timing under Doze, the durable primitive is AlarmManager.setAlarmClock() (leaves low-power mode, delivery time never adjusted; needs SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM or, if GeoWake qualifies as an alarm app, USE_EXACT_ALARM which is auto-granted on install) — not setExactAndAllowWhileIdle (capped to roughly once per 9 minutes in Doze) and not plain jobs/alarms (deferred to maintenance windows). A battery-optimization exemption (REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS) grants network + partial wakelocks in Doze but does not exempt deferred jobs, and Play's Device-and-Network-Abuse policy prohibits requesting it unless core function is adversely affected. Android 16 tightens JobScheduler by enforcing standby-bucket runtime quotas even for jobs running alongside a foreground service and for top-started jobs that continue after the app goes invisible.

## Key facts
- **BOOT_COMPLETED (plus LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED and MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED) is an explicitly documented exemption that lets an app start a foreground service while in the background — subject to per-type restrictions.** — `Allowed exemption: ACTION_BOOT_COMPLETED, ACTION_LOCKED_BOOT_COMPLETED, ACTION_MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18 (covers Android 12–16)) https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start
- **On Android 15+, a BOOT_COMPLETED receiver may NOT launch these FGS types; doing so throws ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. On Android 14+, microphone is additionally banned. location is NOT on the banned list.** — `Banned from BOOT_COMPLETED (A15): dataSync, camera, mediaPlayback, mediaProjection, phoneCall; (A14+) microphone. Exception: ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. adb test flag: FGS_BOOT_COMPLETED_RESTRICTIONS` [high] (as_of Android 15 (API 35)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15
- **FGS types STILL allowed to start from a BOOT_COMPLETED receiver on Android 15 include location, connectedDevice, health, remoteMessaging, specialUse, systemExempted, and shortService.** — `Allowed from BOOT_COMPLETED: location, connectedDevice, health, remoteMessaging, specialUse, systemExempted, shortService` [high] (as_of Android 15 (API 35)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15
- **THE load-bearing gotcha for GeoWake: a `location` foreground service cannot be created while the app is in the background (which includes running from a BOOT_COMPLETED receiver) unless ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION has been granted — otherwise ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. Location runtime permissions are while-in-use.** — `location FGS from background/boot requires ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION granted, else ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18 (Android 14–16)) https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types
- **A location FGS must declare FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION plus foregroundServiceType="location", and hold ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION or ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION at runtime. Missing the type-matching permission throws SecurityException when promoting to foreground (FGS types mandatory since Android 14).** — `<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION"/> + <service android:foregroundServiceType="location"/>; missing perms => SecurityException` [high] (as_of Android 14+ (API 34+)) https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types
- **In Doze, the system defers network access, ignores wakelocks, does not fire standard AlarmManager alarms, and defers JobScheduler/WorkManager jobs, sync adapters, and Wi-Fi scans — batching them into periodic maintenance windows.** — `Deferred in Doze: network, wakelocks (ignored), standard alarms, jobs/WorkManager, syncs, Wi-Fi scans` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby
- **AlarmManager.setAlarmClock() is the most reliable Doze-surviving primitive: the system leaves low-power modes to deliver it and never adjusts its delivery time. It requires an exact-alarm permission (SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM or USE_EXACT_ALARM), unless set via an OnAlarmListener callback.** — `setAlarmClock() exits Doze, exact delivery; needs SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM/USE_EXACT_ALARM (OnAlarmListener path exempt)` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18 (Android 12+)) https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/alarms/schedule
- **setAndAllowWhileIdle() and setExactAndAllowWhileIdle() fire during Doze but are rate-limited to roughly once per 9 minutes per app (the power-limits table phrases the Doze cap as 'while-idle alarms limited to 7 per hour'). They are unsuitable as the sole timing source for a minute-accurate wake alarm.** — `allow-while-idle alarms: ~1 per 9 minutes per app in Doze (power table: 7/hour)` [medium] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby
- **High-priority FCM messages penetrate Doze/App Standby: the system delivers them and grants the app temporary network access and partial wakelocks. Since Android 13 high-priority quota is no longer tied to standby buckets, but the system downgrades senders whose high-priority messages don't produce user notifications.** — `High-priority FCM => temporary network + partial wakelock in Doze; A13+ downgraded if not producing notifications` [high] (as_of Android 13+ (API 33+)) https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details
- **A battery-optimization exemption (REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS / ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS; check via PowerManager.isIgnoringBatteryOptimizations()) lets the app use the network and hold partial wakelocks during Doze/App Standby, but does NOT exempt it from deferred jobs/syncs and (on older APIs) deferred regular alarms.** — `Exemption grants: network + partial wakelocks in Doze/Standby. Does NOT exempt: deferred jobs/syncs; regular alarms still deferred (API 23-)` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby
- **App Standby bucket resource limits (regular jobs / alarms): ACTIVE = 20 min per rolling 60 min, no alarm limit; WORKING_SET = 10 min per 4 h, 10 alarms/hour; FREQUENT = 10 min per 12 h, 2 alarms/hour; RARE = 10 min per 24 h, 1 alarm/hour, network disabled in Doze; RESTRICTED = once/day for 10 min, 1 alarm/day, network disabled.** — `ACTIVE 20m/60m; WORKING_SET 10m/4h + 10 alarms/h; FREQUENT 10m/12h + 2/h; RARE 10m/24h + 1/h; RESTRICTED 10m once/day + 1 alarm/day` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details
- **On Android 13+, an app is dropped into the RESTRICTED bucket after 8 days without user interaction (down from 45 days on Android 12), or if it triggers excessive broadcasts/bindings in a 24h window. RESTRICTED limits apply even while charging.** — `RESTRICTED trigger: 8 days inactivity (A13+); excessive broadcasts/bindings` [high] (as_of Android 13+ (API 33+)) https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby
- **Google Play's Device and Network Abuse policy prohibits apps that 'are not eligible for allowlisting and attempt to bypass system power management.' Requesting direct exemption from Doze/App Standby is only permitted when the app's core function is adversely affected.** — `Play policy: 'Don't bypass System power management unless eligible'` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16559646
- **Google's documented acceptable use cases for requesting direct battery-optimization allowlisting are narrow: messaging/calling apps that cannot use FCM, enterprise VOIP, safety apps that keep users/families safe, task-automation apps, and peripheral-companion apps maintaining a persistent connection. Cases solvable with FCM high-priority messages are 'not acceptable.'** — `Acceptable: non-FCM messaging/calling, enterprise VOIP, safety apps, task automation, persistent peripheral companion` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby
- **ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION must be declared in the manifest (Android 10+) and requested at runtime SEPARATELY from and AFTER foreground location; on earlier versions foreground grant implied background.** — `<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION"/>; incremental request required on API 29+` [high] (as_of Android 10+ (API 29+)) https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions
- **On Android 11 (API 30)+, background location can only be granted from the system Settings page ('Allow all the time') — the runtime dialog has no background button. If an app requests foreground and background location in the same request, the system ignores it and grants neither.** — `A11+: background location = Settings-only ('Allow all the time'); simultaneous fine+background request => system ignores, grants neither` [high] (as_of Android 11+ (API 30+)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/11/privacy/location
- **Google Play requires a Permissions Declaration Form for ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION: App content > Sensitive app permissions > Location permissions. Background location may only be used when it provides significant user benefit relevant to core functionality; a prominent in-app disclosure must appear in normal usage (not buried in a menu) before the runtime prompt; only one background-location feature may be declared; requesting it solely for ads/analytics is prohibited.** — `Play Console: App content > Sensitive app permissions > Location permissions; core-functionality + prominent disclosure + one feature; no ads/analytics` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150
- **Google Play's foreground-service location policy states location-via-FGS must be a continuation of an in-app, user-initiated action and be terminated immediately after that action completes — a tension with keeping a location FGS running through a device reboot.** — `Play FGS-location: 'continuation of an in-app user-initiated action... terminated immediately after'` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241
- **SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM is denied by default for newly installed apps targeting Android 13+ (user must grant it via Settings; check AlarmManager.canScheduleExactAlarms(), request via ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM). USE_EXACT_ALARM is a normal permission auto-granted on install but restricted by Play policy.** — `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM denied-by-default (target A13+); canScheduleExactAlarms(); Intent ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM; USE_EXACT_ALARM auto-granted` [high] (as_of Android 13/14 (API 33/34)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms
- **Play policy limits USE_EXACT_ALARM to apps whose core functionality is an alarm/timer app or a calendar app that shows event notifications; other use cases must use SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM instead. A transit wake-alarm plausibly qualifies as an 'alarm' app, but this is a review judgment.** — `USE_EXACT_ALARM eligible: 'The app is an alarm or timer app' or 'a calendar app that shows event notifications'` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241
- **Android 16 enforces JobScheduler runtime quotas in cases previously exempt: jobs running concurrently with a foreground service, and top-started jobs that continue after the app becomes invisible, now count against the standby-bucket quota; the ACTIVE bucket gains a 20-min-per-60-min cap (previously unlimited). JobInfo.Builder#setImportantWhileForeground() is fully deprecated (ignored).** — `A16: FGS-concurrent jobs + top-started-continuing jobs now quota-bound; ACTIVE bucket capped 20m/60m; setImportantWhileForeground() deprecated. Test flags: OVERRIDE_QUOTA_ENFORCEMENT_TO_FGS_JOBS, OVERRIDE_QUOTA_ENFORCEMENT_TO_TOP_STARTED_JOBS` [high] (as_of Android 16 (API 36)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-all
- **Android 16 confines broadcast priorities to within the same app process; cross-process android:priority / IntentFilter.setPriority() values are no longer respected globally and are clamped to (SYSTEM_LOW_PRIORITY+1 .. SYSTEM_HIGH_PRIORITY-1). Relevant if GeoWake relies on broadcast ordering for boot/alarm receivers.** — `A16: broadcast priority is per-process only; app values clamped, only system can set SYSTEM_LOW/HIGH_PRIORITY` [high] (as_of Android 16 (API 36)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-all
- **Google Play target-API requirements: new apps and updates must currently target Android 15 (API 35). From August 31, 2026, new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36); existing apps must target at least API 35 by August 31, 2026 to reach new users on newer OS versions, with an extension available to November 1, 2026.** — `Now: new/updates target API 35. From 2026-08-31: new/updates target API 36; existing >= API 35. Extension to 2026-11-01` [high] (as_of fetched 2026-07-18) https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk
- **Android 15 added 6-hour-per-24-hour runtime timeouts for dataSync (and mediaProcessing) foreground services via Service.onTimeout(int,int); exceeding it throws a RemoteServiceException and a restart throws ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException ('Time limit already exhausted'). The timer resets when the app is foregrounded. This makes dataSync FGS unsuitable for long-lived background location/timing on A15+.** — `A15 dataSync/mediaProcessing FGS: 6h per 24h cap, onTimeout() callback, restart => ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. Test flag FGS_INTRODUCE_TIME_LIMITS` [high] (as_of Android 15 (API 35)) https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/changes/foreground-service-types
- **Android 12+ location precision: if the user grants only approximate location (ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, ~3 km²) the app cannot get precise location regardless of what it declares; ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION (~50 m) requires a separate upgrade prompt. A transit wake-alarm needs the precise/fine grant to trigger reliably near a stop.** — `A12+: coarse-only grant caps accuracy ~3 km²; fine (~50 m) needs separate upgrade-to-precise request` [high] (as_of Android 12+ (API 31+)) https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions

## Caveats
- The single most important architectural constraint: because BOOT_COMPLETED runs in a background context, GeoWake can only restart a `location` foreground service after reboot IF ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION is already granted. Without it, the boot-restart of a location FGS throws ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException. A pending trip that survives reboot therefore requires (a) background-location grant + Play declaration review, and/or (b) an alternative rearm path using setAlarmClock() to wake the app rather than relying on a continuously-running FGS across the reboot.
- There is a genuine policy tension: Play's FGS-location policy wants location-FGS to be a short 'continuation of a user-initiated action, terminated immediately after,' while a never-miss transit alarm wants a long-lived / reboot-surviving location session. Reconciling this usually means declaring the background-location feature in the Permissions Declaration Form and being able to justify it as core functionality (never-miss wake alarm), not leaning on the foreground-only exemption.
- Doze rate-limits on allow-while-idle alarms are documented two different ways ('once per 9 minutes' in the Doze guide vs '7 per hour' in the power-limits table); treat allow-while-idle as coarse (~10-minute granularity) and use setAlarmClock() when minute-accuracy near the destination is required.
- A battery-optimization exemption helps network + wakelocks but does NOT rescue deferred jobs, and requesting it via ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS risks a Play policy rejection unless GeoWake's core function is demonstrably broken without it. Prefer routing the user to the standard battery-settings screen (ACTION_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATION_SETTINGS) plus reliable setAlarmClock scheduling rather than the direct-exemption intent.
- Chinese/Indian OEM skins common in India (Xiaomi/MIUI-HyperOS, Oppo/Vivo/Realme ColorOS/FuntouchOS, Samsung) apply aggressive proprietary killers beyond stock Doze that are NOT covered by any developer.android.com page fetched here; stock-AOSP survival (verified above) is necessary but not sufficient on those devices — this is out of scope of official Google docs and needs device-specific handling.
- The Android 14 behavior-changes page extraction was thin; the microphone-from-BOOT_COMPLETED ban and location while-in-use rule are corroborated from the Android 15 behavior-changes page and the Foreground service types reference respectively, which are authoritative.

## Open questions
- Does GeoWake's Play listing qualify for USE_EXACT_ALARM as an 'alarm app' (auto-granted, ideal for setAlarmClock) or must it fall back to user-granted SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM? This is a Play-review judgment call, not settled by the policy text alone.
- Will the Play Permissions Declaration reviewers accept 'never-miss transit wake alarm' as core functionality justifying ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION, given the app can also function foreground-only during an active trip? Needs a crafted declaration + demo video.
- For the reboot case specifically: is the intended design to keep a location FGS running across reboot (requires background location), or to persist pending trips and rearm via setAlarmClock/geofence on boot (lighter permission footprint)? The correct architecture depends on this choice.
- Exact per-OEM battery-killer behavior on MIUI/HyperOS, ColorOS, FuntouchOS, One UI for the India device mix — not answerable from Google's docs; needs on-device testing or a source like dontkillmyapp.com.
- Confirm whether GeoWake currently declares FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION and the correct foregroundServiceType, and whether its BOOT_COMPLETED receiver is manifest-declared (the memory note flags a prior dead-backstop-receiver bug under flutter_local_notifications v16+).
