# Legal-by-construction privacy tech for aggregate mobility (for GeoWake, an India Android transit wake-alarm considering an aggregate-mobility data product)

## Summary
"Legal-by-construction" for aggregate mobility means building so that raw trajectories never leave the phone and only differentially-private, k-suppressed aggregate counts are ever released — because the alternative, releasing "anonymised" trajectories, is legally and mathematically indefensible. de Montjoye et al. (Nature, 2013) showed 4 spatio-temporal points re-identify 95% of 1.5M people (2 points >50%), and this exact study is cited in the EU's own regulatory guidance (Article 29 WP Opinion 05/2014) to demonstrate that pseudonymised location data is not anonymous. That same EU opinion's Table 6 is the design keystone: k-anonymity alone defeats "singling out" but still permits linkability and inference, whereas differential privacy can address all three if properly engineered — so k-anonymity is a necessary floor, not a sufficient control, for mobility data. Real deployments converge on a recipe you can copy: on-device/federated aggregation with secure aggregation (Google Federated Analytics; Apple local DP), suppression of sparse cells (Google's COVID Mobility Reports discard any cell with fewer than 100 contributing users and publish nothing below 3 km²), and Laplace/Gaussian noise on released counts with a bounded per-user daily epsilon (Google Mobility capped each user's contribution so each metric had ε=0.44, ≤1.76/day total). For an India product this matters because the DPDP Act 2023 applies only to digital personal data tied to an identifiable individual — irreversibly anonymised aggregates fall outside, and Section 17(2)(b) additionally exempts statistical/research processing that is not used to make decisions about a specific person. The catch: epsilon is not comparable across systems (Apple uses ε=2–8 in the local model; the 2020 US Census set ε=19.61 in the central model), so "we use differential privacy" is meaningless without stating the model, the epsilon, and the per-user contribution bound.

## Key facts
- **de Montjoye et al. 'Unique in the Crowd' showed that in a mobility dataset with location at antenna resolution and hourly time, four spatio-temporal points are enough to uniquely identify 95% of individuals; two points single out more than 50%. Dataset: 15 months of data for 1.5 million people. This is the canonical proof that an 'anonymised trajectory' is a fiction.** — `4 points -> 95% unique; 2 points -> >50%; N=1.5M over 15 months` [high] (as_of 2013-03-25) https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01376
- **The same paper derives that the uniqueness of mobility traces decays only as roughly the 1/10 power (exponent ~0.1) of their spatial/temporal resolution — i.e., coarsening the grid or time bins provides very little additional anonymity. This is why generalization/aggregation of trajectory microdata cannot rescue anonymity on its own.** — `uniqueness ~ resolution^(-0.1)` [high] (as_of 2013-03-25) https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01376
- **The EU Article 29 Working Party Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques (WP216) explicitly cites the de Montjoye MIT study in its 'Locations' section, restating that '95% of the population could be singled-out with four location points, and that just two points were enough to single-out more than 50%' even when identities were pseudonymised — using it to conclude pseudonymised location data is not anonymised. Regulators treat this result as authoritative.** [high] (as_of 2014-04-10) https://iapp.org/media/pdf/resource_center/wp216_Anonymisation-Techniques_04-2014.pdf
- **WP216 evaluates every anonymisation technique against three re-identification risks — singling out, linkability, inference (Table 6). Aggregation/K-anonymity: singling out = No (protected), linkability = Yes (still a risk), inference = Yes (still a risk). Differential privacy: all three = 'May not' be a risk (i.e., can be addressed if properly applied). Pseudonymisation: Yes/Yes/Yes (not anonymisation). This is the design rule: k-anonymity is a necessary floor but insufficient alone for mobility; DP is required to also blunt linkability and inference.** — `K-anon: singling-out=No, linkability=Yes, inference=Yes | DP: may-not/may-not/may-not` [high] (as_of 2014-04-10) https://iapp.org/media/pdf/resource_center/wp216_Anonymisation-Techniques_04-2014.pdf
- **WP216 gives concrete k-anonymity engineering guidance: higher k = stronger guarantees; 'if k=2 then the probability for the two individuals to share the same property is higher than for k>10'; k must be high enough that no single record is too large a fraction of a cluster; and inflating k by dropping quasi-identifiers is a critical mistake. It does not mandate a single number — parameters must be chosen case-by-case — which is why practitioners commonly target k in the tens-to-hundreds for sparse spatio-temporal cells.** — `avoid k<=2; prefer k>10; no fixed statutory k` [high] (as_of 2014-04-10) https://iapp.org/media/pdf/resource_center/wp216_Anonymisation-Techniques_04-2014.pdf
- **Google's COVID-19 Community Mobility Reports add Laplace noise (Google's open-source DP library) to counts. Each user's contribution is bounded to 4 (category,location) pairs per day per geographic level (excess randomly discarded), so each daily place-visit metric is protected with epsilon=0.44 and each user's total daily contribution is at most epsilon=1.76. Per-granularity Laplace scales/epsilons: level 0 and 1 = 1/0.11 (approx 9.09, epsilon=0.11), level 2 = 1/0.22 (approx 4.55, epsilon=0.22). This is a concrete, copyable pattern for bounding per-user contribution to an O-D/flow aggregate.** — `epsilon=0.44 per place-visit; <=1.76/day total; contribution capped at 4 pairs/day` [high] (as_of 2020-04-09) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.04145v2
- **Google's Mobility Reports also apply hard suppression thresholds on top of DP: any metric whose differentially-private count of contributing users (after noise) is below 100 is discarded, and no metrics are published for any geographic region smaller than 3 km^2 (small regions may be merged to clear the 3 km^2 floor). This is a real-world k~=100 minimum-cell-size rule combined with a minimum spatial-cell area — directly applicable to station x hour flow suppression.** — `suppress if DP contributing-user count < 100; no cell < 3 km^2` [high] (as_of 2020-04-09) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.04145v2
- **Google Federated Analytics is the reference on-device aggregation pattern: 'only the user has a copy of their data' and only aggregated results — 'never any data from a particular device' — are made available; Secure Aggregation cryptographically masks each device's values so the server can decrypt only the combined tally 'and nothing else', not any individual contribution. In production in Gboard, Android Messages smart-reply, and Pixel Now Playing. This is how you keep raw trajectories on the phone by construction.** [high] (as_of 2020-05-27) https://research.google/blog/federated-analytics-collaborative-data-science-without-data-collection/
- **Apple uses LOCAL differential privacy — noise is injected on-device before data leaves the phone (Count Mean Sketch / Hadamard Count Mean Sketch; each coordinate flipped with probability 1/(1+e^(epsilon/2))), donations carry no identifier, IP addresses are dropped, and data is retained at most 3 months. Published per-donation privacy budgets: emoji epsilon=4 (1 donation/day); Lookup Hints epsilon=4 (2/day); QuickType epsilon=8 (2/day); Health types epsilon=2 (1/day); Safari high-energy/crash domains epsilon=4; Safari autoplay-intent epsilon=8 (Safari capped at 2 donations/day). Shows the local-model tradeoff: larger epsilon than central-model releases.** — `emoji e=4; LookupHints e=4; QuickType e=8; Health e=2; Safari e=4/e=8; <=3 mo retention` [high] (as_of 2017 (iOS 11 / macOS High Sierra era; current published version)) https://www.apple.com/privacy/docs/Differential_Privacy_Overview.pdf
- **The US Census Bureau 2020 Disclosure Avoidance System (TopDown) is the largest central-model DP deployment. Final production privacy-loss budget for the Redistricting (P.L. 94-171) data was epsilon=19.61 total (persons file epsilon=17.14, housing-unit data epsilon=2.47), announced 9 June 2021. The noisy-measurement mechanism is the discrete Gaussian under zero-concentrated DP (zCDP), with the released allocation corresponding to rho=3.65. The very large epsilon (vs Google's ~0.44) illustrates that epsilon values are NOT comparable across deployments or across local vs central models.** — `redistricting epsilon=19.61 (persons 17.14 + housing 2.47); zCDP rho=3.65; discrete Gaussian` [high] (as_of 2021-06-09) https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2021/2020-census-key-parameters.html
- **OnTheMap (US Census LEHD) is the first production deployment of a formal differential-privacy model and it protects exactly an origin-destination commuting matrix — schema (worker id, origin block, destination block) — by releasing synthetic/noise-protected block-level commuting flows so that individual worker home locations are never disclosed (Machanavajjhala, Kifer, Abowd, Gehrke, Vilhuber, 'Privacy: Theory meets Practice on the Map', ICDE 2008, using probabilistic differential privacy). Precedent that a legally-defensible O-D flow product is achievable via DP synthesis rather than releasing trips.** — `first production DP deployment (2008); protects O-D commuting matrix; probabilistic (epsilon,delta)-DP` [medium] (as_of 2008) https://lehd.ces.census.gov/doc/help/ICDE08_conference_0768.pdf
- **GDPR Recital 26 is the legal basis for 'legal-by-construction': data-protection principles 'do not apply to anonymous information ... or to personal data rendered anonymous in such a manner that the data subject is not or no longer identifiable.' Identifiability must account for 'all the means reasonably likely to be used, such as singling out,' considering cost, time and available technology. Because de Montjoye shows singling-out of trajectories is cheap, only DP-aggregated, non-trajectory outputs plausibly clear this bar.** [high] (as_of GDPR in force 2018-05-25) https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/
- **India's DPDP Act 2023 applies only to 'digital personal data' about an individual 'identifiable by or in relation to such data' (identifiability standard, GDPR-like). Irreversibly anonymised aggregates that carry no reasonable re-identification risk fall outside its scope. Section 17(2)(b) additionally exempts processing for research, archiving or statistical purposes, provided it is not used to make any decision specific to a data principal (and is done per prescribed standards). This is the statutory hook that makes a DP-aggregated mobility product low-risk in India — but the carve-out depends on the anonymisation actually being robust.** — `scope = digital personal data (identifiability); Sec 17(2)(b) research/statistical exemption` [medium] (as_of Act No. 22 of 2023; assented 2023-08-11) https://fpf.org/blog/the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-of-india-explained/
- **Reinforcing evidence that sparse behavioural traces are uniquely identifying: de Montjoye et al. 'Unique in the Shopping Mall' (Science, 2015) found 4 spatio-temporal points re-identify 90% of 1.1M people in 3 months of credit-card metadata, and knowing a transaction's price raises re-identification risk by ~22%. Confirms the 4-points result generalizes beyond mobile-phone location to any timestamped-location stream — including transit tap/GPS events.** — `4 points -> 90% re-identified; N=1.1M over 3 months; price adds ~22%` [medium] (as_of 2015-01-30) https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1256297

## Caveats
- Epsilon values are NOT comparable across systems: Google's Community Mobility uses epsilon=0.44 per metric in the CENTRAL/trusted-curator model, Apple uses epsilon=2-8 per donation in the LOCAL model (much weaker per-unit but data never leaves the device cleartext), and the 2020 US Census set epsilon=19.61 in the central model. Any 'we use differential privacy' claim is meaningless unless it states the model (local vs central), the epsilon, and the per-user contribution bound.
- k-anonymity ALONE is legally and technically insufficient for mobility/trajectory data. Per WP216 Table 6 it stops 'singling out' but not linkability or inference, and per de Montjoye trajectories are uniquely identifying with as few as 2-4 points. Use k-suppression only as a floor UNDER differential-privacy-noised, non-trajectory aggregate counts — never release trips or per-user paths, k-anonymised or not.
- Anonymisation is contextual and risk-based, not a binary switch. Both GDPR Recital 26 ('means reasonably likely to be used') and India's DPDP require ongoing re-assessment as auxiliary data and technology change; WP216 explicitly warns against the 'release and forget' approach and requires periodic re-evaluation of residual risk.
- India's DPDP Act 2023 does not contain an explicit statutory definition of, or safe-harbour for, 'anonymised data' the way GDPR Recital 26 does; the out-of-scope status is inferred from the 'identifiable individual' definition. If re-identification is reasonably possible, the aggregates are treated as personal data and the full Act applies. The Section 17(2)(b) statistical exemption also still requires 'prescribed standards' that are set by the DPDP Rules.
- The de Montjoye 1/10-power uniqueness formula and the 95%/4-point figure are specific to that carrier dataset's resolution; exact re-identification rates vary with population density, sampling and grid size. The qualitative conclusion (trajectory microdata cannot be safely 'anonymised' by coarsening) is robust and regulator-endorsed; the precise percentages are dataset-dependent.
- OnTheMap epsilon values quoted from secondary parsing of the ICDE 2008 paper are approximate; treat 'first production DP deployment protecting an O-D commuting matrix' as the load-bearing, high-confidence fact and verify the exact epsilon/mechanism against the primary PDF before citing a number.

## Open questions
- What exact (epsilon, k, contribution-cap, minimum-cell-area) should GeoWake pick for a station x hour x flow O-D product? Google's shipped defaults (per-user cap -> epsilon<=1.76/day, suppress cells <100 users, no cell <3 km^2) are a strong copyable starting point, but the right values depend on India transit density and how often a single rider contributes.
- Have the DPDP Rules 2025 that operationalize the Act (including the Section 17(2)(b) 'prescribed standards' for statistical/research processing and any data-breach/consent-manager duties) been finalised and notified, and what do they require of an anonymised-aggregate product? This changed within the last 18 months and needs a primary-source check against the notified Rules text before relying on the statistical exemption.
- Does GeoWake actually need to EXPORT any aggregate mobility data at all? The app is already on-device by design; if no aggregate product is shipped externally, the entire re-identification and DP-release surface disappears and the legal analysis collapses to 'no personal data leaves the phone' (the strongest legal-by-construction posture). The privacy tech above is only needed if a data product or B2B/transit-agency feed is a real business goal.
- Confirm the exact OnTheMap/LEHD epsilon and mechanism (probabilistic DP vs the later moving to a synthetic-data model) from the primary ICDE 2008 PDF and current LEHD documentation if it will be cited to an investor or transit-agency partner as O-D precedent.
