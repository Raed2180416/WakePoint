# Maximum design and operational speeds (km/h) of Indian urban + regional rail lines, for GeoWake's never-late V_LINE overbound

## Summary
For GeoWake's never-late bound, Indian urban/regional rail splits into three speed tiers. TIER 1 (regional, must overbound highest): Namo Bharat / Delhi–Meerut RRTS — design 180 km/h, operational 160 km/h — is by far the fastest and the single line that breaks the metro envelope; a safe V_LINE for any RRTS corridor is 180 (use ~190 for margin). TIER 2: Delhi Airport Express (Orange) — CAF trainset design 135 km/h, operational 120 km/h since Sep 2023 (ignore the 350 km/h "signalling" marketing claim — it is not a physical train speed); and Mumbai Suburban local EMUs — newest Medha/Bombardier rakes capable of ~120 km/h, system rated ~110 km/h. TIER 3: essentially EVERY conventional Indian metro (Delhi regular lines, Mumbai Metro, Bengaluru Purple/Green/Yellow, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata incl. underground, Kochi, Ahmedabad, Pune L1/L2, Nagpur, Lucknow, Noida Aqua, Gurugram Rapid) runs a maximum operational 80 km/h on a ~90 km/h design speed — Chennai is officially "design 90, operational 80," and this 90/80 pattern is the standard across Indian standard-gauge metros. Practical GeoWake overbounds: 100 km/h covers every conventional metro with margin; 140 km/h covers Airport Express + Mumbai suburban; 190 km/h is required only on RRTS/Namo Bharat corridors. Recent (last 18 months) changes to watch: RRTS operational 160 km/h now live across the full Delhi–Meerut corridor, Airport Express bumped to 120 km/h (Sep 2023), and Pune's PPP Line 3 opened (Jun 2025) at 85 km/h operational (~95 design).

## Caveats
- FASTEST LINE FLAG: Namo Bharat / Delhi–Meerut RRTS (design 180, operational 160 km/h) is the single line that breaks the metro speed envelope. Airport Express is second (design 135, operational 120). Any GeoWake never-late bound applied uniformly at a metro-tier value (e.g. 100 km/h) WILL be violated on RRTS and on Airport Express — these need their own higher V_LINE.
- Do NOT use the Airport Express '350 km/h' figure as a physical speed. It is a marketing/theoretical claim about the RHEDA-2000 slab-track+signalling headroom, not the trainset's capability. The CAF trainset design speed is 135 km/h and operational is 120 km/h. Using 350 would make the never-late bound useless (hugely over-conservative).
- 'Design speed' vs 'operational max' matters for overbound direction: to NEVER be late by physics, V_LINE must be >= the true top achievable speed, i.e. use the DESIGN speed (or the highest rake capability), never the average or the operational cap. For most Indian metros design=90 while operational=80, so bounding at operational-80 would UNDER-bound by 10 km/h if a train is ever cleared to design speed.
- The 90 km/h design / 80 km/h operational pattern is only officially confirmed (operator source) for Chennai and via DPR for Ahmedabad/Pune. For Hyderabad, Kolkata, Lucknow, Noida, Namma, Gurugram, Mumbai Metro and Delhi regular lines the '80 operational' is well-sourced but the '~90 design' is inferred from the Indian SG-metro norm (medium confidence) rather than a per-line operator datasheet.
- Mumbai Suburban max speed is rake-dependent and heterogeneous on the same line (85 / 100 / 110–120 km/h). Because you cannot know which rake the user boards, overbound the whole suburban network at 120 km/h.
- Pune Line 3 is a different beast from Pune L1/L2 — it is a PPP corridor (Siemens/Tata) that opened Jun 2025 and runs faster (85 km/h operational). One secondary source (trak.in) headlined '120 kmph'; CMRS approval and PMRDA/Wikipedia indicate 85 operational / ~95 design. Treat 120 as unverified; if you want zero-risk overbound on L3, use ~120.
- Nagpur has TWO systems: the running standard-gauge Nagpur Metro (max ~90 km/h) and a separate PLANNED Nagpur Broad-Gauge/regional Metro (design 200, operating 160 km/h). If GeoWake ever tags a Nagpur regional corridor, it falls in the RRTS tier, not the metro tier.
