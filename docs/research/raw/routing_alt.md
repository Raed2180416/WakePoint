# Escaping Google Directions cost for GeoWake by self-hosting OSM routing (OSRM/Valhalla + OTP) and tiles, at India / 100K-MAU scale

## Summary
India is the entire game-changer here: the Geofabrik India OSM extract is only 1.6 GB, so GeoWake never needs the planet-scale hardware (100+ GB RAM) that scares people off self-hosting. India car+foot routing with OSRM fits in ~3–4 GB RAM on a single small VM. A production self-host stack (OSRM for car/foot + OpenTripPlanner 2 for metro GTFS + Protomaps PMTiles basemap) costs roughly $90/mo minimal to $300/mo for an HA pair — versus a Google routing bill that, at 100K MAU and ~5M routing calls/month, runs ~$7,400/mo on Google's discounted India pricing or ~$15,550/mo on global pricing. That is a 95–98% infrastructure cost cut, with the real cost shifting to one-time engineering and pipeline maintenance. Two immediate, near-zero-effort levers: (1) make sure GeoWake is billed as an India-based Google Maps customer (INR billing, ~70% lower Routes pricing since Aug 2024) as a hedge/bridge, and (2) migrate off the now-Legacy Directions API to the Routes API regardless. All the open-source pieces (OSRM BSD, Valhalla MIT, OTP2, Protomaps) are free for commercial use; the only binding obligation is ODbL attribution — display '© OpenStreetMap contributors' linking to osm.org/copyright. The single biggest gap is not cost or compute but transit-data coverage: Delhi publishes real DMRC metro GTFS, but GTFS is patchy across other Indian metros.

## Key facts
- **Google moved to per-SKU free tiers and Legacy status for old APIs in March 2025: the flat $200/month credit was removed and replaced by per-SKU free monthly quotas, and the Directions API (plus Places and Distance Matrix) was designated Legacy, with the Routes API as its successor. Volume discounts now scale out to 5,000,000+ monthly events.** — `Directions API = Legacy; use Routes API. $200 credit gone; free = per-SKU quota. Effective 2025-03-01.` [high] (as_of 2026-07-15) https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/march-2025
- **Google Routes API global 'Compute Routes' pricing is volume-tiered: Essentials $5.00/1k (0–100k), $4.00/1k (100k–500k), $3.00/1k (500k+); Pro $10/$8/$6; with 10,000 free Essentials events/month. At 5M calls/month this is ~$15,550/mo (90k×$5 + 400k×$4 + ~4.5M×$3).** — `Essentials $5/$4/$3 per 1k; 10k free/mo` [high] (as_of 2026-07-15) https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing
- **Google offers a separate, much cheaper India pricing list (billed in INR, up to ~70% lower, available since Aug 2024). Routes 'Compute Routes' Essentials in India = $1.50/1k up to 5M events then $0.38/1k beyond, with 70,000 free events/month. Legacy Directions India is priced identically. At 5M calls/month this is ~$7,395/mo — roughly half the global cost.** — `India Routes Essentials: $1.50/1k (<5M), $0.38/1k (5M+), 70k free/mo` [high] (as_of 2026-07-15) https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing-india
- **The Geofabrik India OSM extract (india-latest.osm.pbf) is only ~1.6 GB, current to 2026-07-15. This is ~2.6% of the ~61 GB planet PBF, which is why India-scale self-hosted routing needs tiny hardware compared with planet-scale deployments.** — `India OSM = 1.6 GB PBF` [high] (as_of 2026-07-15) https://download.geofabrik.de/asia/india.html
- **OSRM planet-scale serving needs ~123 GiB RAM for the car profile (CH), with preprocessing disk in the hundreds of GB (per official wiki, v5.26 on a 61 GiB PBF); requirements scale roughly linearly with input size. Extrapolating linearly, the 1.6 GB India extract needs only ~3–4 GB RAM to serve car routing (foot profile ~2x), fitting comfortably on an 8 GB VM.** — `Planet car serve ~123 GiB RAM → India ~3–4 GB RAM (linear extrapolation)` [medium] (as_of 2021-11 (wiki data), extrapolation 2026) https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Disk-and-Memory-Requirements
- **Throughput/latency (2026 benchmark): OSRM ~5,000–10,000 queries/sec and 5–10 ms latency on a single 8-core server; Valhalla ~2,000–4,000 q/s (10–30 ms); GraphHopper ~1,000–3,000 q/s. OSRM is fastest for car; Valhalla is tile-based (~100 GB disk planet, moderate RAM) and offers native multimodal/costing flexibility in one engine (MIT license). For India's tiny graph, OSRM's simplicity wins; Valhalla is the pick only if you want car+foot+bike+time-based costing (and optionally transit) unified in one service.** — `OSRM 5–10k qps; Valhalla 2–4k qps; GraphHopper 1–3k qps` [medium] (as_of 2026-04-25) https://www.pistack.xyz/posts/2026-04-25-graphhopper-vs-osrm-vs-valhalla-self-hosted-routing-engines-guide-2026/
- **OpenTripPlanner 2 memory scales with data: <1 GB to 100+ GB (e.g. all-Germany 95 GB, Finland ~10 GB). For GeoWake, OTP is only needed for metro/transit legs, so a metro-region GTFS + local street graph is a few GB — cohabits the same VM. OTP2 supports splitting graph-build from serving to cap runtime RAM.** — `OTP metro-only India graph ≈ low single-digit GB RAM` [high] (as_of 2026 (OTP2 docs)) https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/Basic-Tutorial/
- **Delhi's Open Transit Data portal publishes real static GTFS for Delhi Metro (DMRC) and buses, plus GTFS-realtime vehicle positions (API-key gated). This is directly consumable by OTP2 for the metro leg. Caveat: metro GTFS coverage is uneven across India — Kochi Metro was the first Indian agency to publish open GTFS (2020); many metros still lack public feeds, in which case fall back to OSM rail-line geometry (which GeoWake already uses).** — `Delhi = DMRC metro GTFS + bus GTFS + realtime available` [high] (as_of 2026-07) https://otd.delhi.gov.in/documentation/
- **Concrete India-region hosting: AWS Lightsail Mumbai (ap-south-1) flat-rate Linux bundles — 4 GB/2 vCPU/80 GB = $24/mo, 8 GB/2 vCPU/160 GB = $44/mo, 16 GB/4 vCPU/320 GB = $84/mo (instance price identical in Mumbai; only bundled data transfer is halved). A single 16 GB Mumbai box runs OSRM car+foot + OTP metro; HA pair + LB + backups ≈ $200–300/mo.** — `Lightsail Mumbai: 8 GB=$44/mo, 16 GB=$84/mo` [high] (as_of 2026-07) https://aws.amazon.com/lightsail/pricing/
- **Cheapest non-India option: Hetzner Cloud CX32 = 4 vCPU / 8 GB / 80 GB for €6.80/mo — ample for India-scale OSRM, but Hetzner has no India datacenter (nearest is Singapore), adding latency for the initial route call. Note: Hetzner raised dedicated-vCPU (CCX) prices 2.1x–2.7x on 15 June 2026; shared CX plans stayed cheap.** — `Hetzner CX32 = €6.80/mo (no India DC)` [high] (as_of 2026-06-15) https://www.hetzner.com/pressroom/new-cx-plans/
- **Basemap tiles: Protomaps (PMTiles) packages the whole India basemap as a single file served via HTTP range requests directly from object storage (S3 / Cloudflare R2) with no tile-server process — near-zero compute, and on Cloudflare R2 (zero egress fees) effectively ~$0–5/mo. It is open-source and OSM/ODbL-based. Hosted Protomaps API is free under 1M tiles/mo for non-commercial use; commercial hosted access starts ~$14/mo. This avoids the per-MAU/per-tile metering of hosted map SDKs.** — `Protomaps PMTiles self-host ≈ $0–5/mo on R2` [medium] (as_of 2026-07) https://protomaps.com/
- **Hosted-tile alternatives if you don't self-host PMTiles: Stadia Maps (uses Valhalla for its routing) — Free 200k credits/mo (no commercial use), Starter $20/mo for 1M credits, Standard $80/mo for 7.5M, Pro $250/mo for 25M; standard basemap tile = 1 credit, a routing request = 20 credits. MapTiler Cloud offers similar tier'd plans and an On-Prem license for >500 end-users. For 100K MAU these hosted plans meter fast, which is why self-hosted PMTiles is the cost winner.** — `Stadia: Starter $20/1M credits; tile=1 credit, route=20 credits` [high] (as_of 2026-07) https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/
- **Licensing is not a blocker for a commercial app: OSM data is ODbL; OSRM (BSD-2), Valhalla (MIT), OpenTripPlanner and Protomaps are open-source and free for commercial use with no per-request fees. The one binding obligation is ODbL attribution — display '© OpenStreetMap contributors' where users see the map (map corner, About screen, or startup splash), with 'OpenStreetMap' linking to openstreetmap.org/copyright; it may be collapsible/dismissible.** — `ODbL: must show '© OpenStreetMap contributors' + link to osm.org/copyright` [high] (as_of 2026-07) https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines
- **Bottom-line comparison at 100K MAU (parameterized on routing calls/month, since GeoWake computes a route mainly at trip-start then tracks locally). At ~5M calls/mo: Google India ≈ $7,395/mo, Google global ≈ $15,550/mo, self-host ≈ $90/mo (single box) to $300/mo (HA). Unit economics on Google India = $0.0015/routing call = $0.015/user/mo if each user makes 10 calls. Self-hosting converts a $90k–$187k/yr recurring routing bill into ~$1k–$4k/yr infra plus one-time engineering.** — `5M calls/mo: Google India $7.4k vs Google global $15.5k vs self-host $0.09k–$0.3k /mo` [medium] (as_of 2026-07) https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing-india

## Caveats
- The 100K-MAU dollar figures depend entirely on routing calls/month, which I estimated (1.5M / 5M / 10M scenarios) — I do not have GeoWake's actual per-user Directions call pattern. Because the app already does local reachability/EKF on OSM graphs and tracks locally after trip start, real Directions volume may be low (route + ETA computed once per trip), which shrinks the absolute Google bill and lengthens the payback period on self-hosting engineering effort. Plug your real calls/user/month into the unit economics ($0.0015/call India, $0.003–$0.005/call global) before deciding.
- For a map app the larger Google bill is often the Maps SDK / dynamic-map loads and Geocoding, NOT Directions. Self-hosting routing alone may not be the biggest saving — evaluate tile/basemap and geocoding costs in the same exercise (Protomaps PMTiles for tiles; Nominatim or Pelias self-hosted for geocoding, both India-scale-cheap).
- The OSRM ~3–4 GB India RAM figure is a linear extrapolation from planet numbers, not a measured India build — validate by actually building the India car+foot graph before sizing the VM. It will be small, but confirm.
- The real cost of self-hosting is engineering + operations, not servers: building and maintaining the OSM→OSRM/Valhalla→OTP pipeline, monthly graph rebuilds from fresh OSM, monitoring, HA/failover, and on-call. Budget this honestly; it is where the '$90/mo' understates true TCO.
- Google's India pricing (INR billing, ~70% off) requires the billing account to qualify as India-based. If GeoWake's Google Cloud billing entity is not registered in India, you're on the higher global rates — confirm which list applies to your account.
- Some 2026 figures (routing-engine throughput, Protomaps commercial pricing) come from vendor/community blogs rather than primary docs; treat throughput as order-of-magnitude, not SLA.
- Transit-data licensing varies per city feed (Delhi OTD, Kochi, Bangalore/BMTC community feeds each have their own terms and update cadence) — check each GTFS feed's license and freshness before shipping it in a paid app.

## Open questions
- What is GeoWake's actual Directions/Routes call volume per active user per month? This single number decides whether self-hosting pays back in weeks or is premature optimization.
- Is the Google Cloud billing account registered as India-based (INR / ~70%-off pricing)? If not, switching may be the fastest ~50% cost cut with zero engineering.
- Which Indian cities must GeoWake support at launch, and do their metros publish open GTFS? Delhi (DMRC) does; many do not — for those you need the OSM rail-geometry fallback rather than OTP.
- Does GeoWake actually need turn-by-turn routing, or only route polyline + ETA + station geometry for the wake trigger? If the latter, OSRM's /route with a coarse profile (or even precomputed OSM rail geometry) may suffice and OTP may be optional except for multi-leg metro itineraries.
- Do you want one unified engine (Valhalla: car+foot+bike+transit costing) to reduce operational surface, or best-of-breed (OSRM for road speed + OTP2 for metro correctness)? Valhalla trades some road throughput for a single deployable.
- Where will the initial route call be served from for latency — AWS Mumbai (in-country, ~$44–84/mo) vs Hetzner Singapore/EU (cheaper but +50–150 ms)? For a wake-alarm computed once at trip start, EU latency may be acceptable and cheaper.
