# The commercial aggregate origin-destination (O-D) / footfall data market GeoWake could sell station-granularity transit-intent data into

## Summary
There are two distinct buyer markets, sourced by two distinct data types. (1) A commercial "location intelligence" market (~USD 24-25B in 2025, growing 13-16%/yr) where Placer.ai, Advan (ex-SafeGraph Patterns), Unacast/Gravy, Veraset and Cuebiq sell footfall, catchment/trade-area and device-level GPS movement data — almost all sourced from SDKs embedded in third-party mobile apps, and sold mainly to retail, real estate, hedge funds and advertisers via annual subscriptions (Placer ~USD 5k-30k/yr) or bulk data licenses. (2) A transportation-planning market where StreetLight (owned by Jacobs since 2022) and Replica sell modeled origin-destination matrices to DOTs, MPOs and transit agencies as SaaS subscriptions — a real US public-agency O-D subscription runs ~USD 20k-45k/yr (Teton County paid USD 42,888 for one year, of which O-D-for-modeling alone was USD 19,600). In India the buyers are city development authorities and transit corporations procuring Comprehensive Mobility Plans (MoHUA ToR) through consultants, historically relying on expensive one-off household/intercept O-D surveys and outdated data; telco CDR is the incumbent big-data source but operators resist sharing. GeoWake's differentiator — a device that is actually travelling AND carries a rider-declared destination label — removes the trip-end and trip-purpose inference that raw GPS panels must model, but its tiny panel, India's DPDP consent/anonymisation regime, and slow price-sensitive public procurement make this a slow B2B2G/research play, not a fast ad-tech revenue stream, and only aggregate O-D above k-anonymity thresholds should ever be sold.

- **Replica (spun out of Alphabet/Sidewalk Labs, independent 2019) builds a synthetic activity-based travel-demand model from de-identified mobile data — ~1.2B trips / 329M people across 48 states — sold to public agencies (Caltrans, TxDOT, Illinois DOT, MTA) and firms (Waymo, AECOM, Arup, WSP); its pricing scales with population (additional agencies added at ~0.15 x city/served population).** — `Synthetic O-D model; pricing ~0.15 x population per added agency` [medium] (as_of 2025) https://www.replicahq.com/pricing
- **In India, city-level O-D demand is procured through Comprehensive Mobility Plans (CMPs): MoHUA issues the Terms of Reference and a five-stage methodology, plans are prepared by development authorities / municipal corporations (usually via consultants), and they rely heavily on one-off household and intercept O-D surveys and 'outdated and incomplete data' — the exact gap continuous passive data fills.** — `MoHUA CMP ToR; consultant-led; survey-dependent, data often stale` [high] (as_of 2024) https://www.orfonline.org/research/comprehensive-mobility-planning-in-indian-cities-challenges-gaps-and-the-way-forward
- **NITI Aayog's official 'Data-Driven Mobility' framing recognises mobility data as key to efficient passenger transport; the main big-data source (telco CDR from mobile network operators) is hampered by operators' strong historical reluctance to share, leaving a gap that app-based OD data could fill for planners and city agencies.** — `CDR incumbent but operators withhold; NITI Aayog endorses data-driven mobility` [medium] (as_of 2023) https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-02/Mobility-data.pdf
- **India's DPDP Act 2023 with DPDP Rules 2025 (notified by MeitY on Nov 13, 2025; broad compliance expected ~mid-2027) governs digital personal data; irreversibly anonymised data falls outside the Act's core obligations, but the Act does NOT define the technical bar for anonymisation, and collecting location data requires verifiable consent — so only aggregate/anonymised O-D above safe thresholds is defensibly sellable.** — `Rules notified 13 Nov 2025; anonymised data out-of-scope but bar undefined; consent required to collect` [high] (as_of Nov 2025) https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/decoding-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023
- **The overall location-intelligence market is ~USD 24-25B in 2025 growing ~13-16%/yr (Grand View ~USD 24.2B/15.5% CAGR; Mordor ~USD 25.1B/13.45% CAGR) — the addressable pool, though the transport-planning O-D niche GeoWake targets is a small fraction dominated by StreetLight and Replica.** — `~USD 24-25B (2025), 13-16% CAGR` [medium] (as_of 2025) https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/location-intelligence-market
- **Academic consensus is that passively-collected GPS/big-data avoids the bias of stated-preference surveys because it captures actual (revealed) behaviour; GeoWake's edge is therefore NOT 'stated beats GPS' but that it uniquely pairs revealed travel with a rider-DECLARED destination, eliminating the trip-end and trip-purpose inference that raw ping panels (Placer/Veraset/Advan) must model.** — `Labeled O-D removes trip-end/purpose inference of raw GPS` [medium] (as_of 2025) https://tfresource.org/topics/Stated_preference_surveys.html

## Caveats
- Scale is the whole game and GeoWake starts with almost none. Every incumbent's product rests on tens of millions of devices (Placer) or billions of daily pings (Veraset); aggregate O-D and footfall are only saleable above k-anonymity thresholds, so a small panel means most station-pair cells are un-sellable (too few riders) — a hard cold-start problem for a wake-alarm app.
- The high-signal asset (the destination a rider set) is also the most privacy-sensitive. Under DPDP 2023/Rules 2025, the destination label is personal data at collection and needs verifiable consent; only irreversibly aggregated O-D can be sold, and the Act leaves the anonymisation bar undefined. Selling anything device-level is the Veraset/EFF landmine — reputationally fatal for a consumer alarm app.
- India public procurement is slow, consultant-mediated and deeply price-sensitive. US anchors (StreetLight ~USD 43k/yr for one county; Placer USD 5-30k/yr) will not transfer 1:1 — Indian transit authorities and CMP consultants pay a fraction, buy episodically per-plan, and default to cheap surveys or free telco/census data. Realistic monetization is B2B2G via consultants (WRI India, AECOM, WSP) or direct to metro/BMTC-type authorities and academic 'Data for Good', not high-margin ad-tech ARR.
- The most valuable comparable (Replica) proves the model works but also shows the moat: it needed Alphabet-scale data + a synthetic-population modeling stack to sell to DOTs. A footfall/retail play (Placer/Advan lane) is even more scale-gated and US-centric; GeoWake would compete on India station-level granularity it uniquely has, not on volume.
- Some figures (Placer USD 5-30k list price, Replica 0.15 x population formula, market-size totals) are analyst/marketplace estimates or vendor pages, not audited primary numbers; the Teton County USD 42,888 and Jacobs/Advan/merger dates are the hardest-sourced facts here.
- This synthesizes public sources only; it is market intelligence, not legal advice. Any actual data-sale product for India needs a DPDP-specific legal review of the consent notice, the anonymisation method, and cross-border transfer rules before a single dataset is sold.

## Open questions
- What is the true k-anonymized sellable coverage at GeoWake's projected panel size — i.e. how many station-pair O-D cells clear a minimum-count threshold in Bengaluru/Delhi/Mumbai before the data is commercially meaningful?
- Who is the actual first paying buyer in India: a transit corporation (BMRCL/DMRC/BMTC), a CMP consultant, a Smart Cities ICCC, an academic 'Data for Good' partner, or a retail/real-estate footfall buyer — and what will each realistically pay in INR?
- Does GeoWake's current consent flow (permissions for the alarm) legally cover secondary use of aggregated destination data for sale under DPDP Rules 2025, or does it need a separate, unbundled consent notice?
- Are there live Indian public tenders (GeM / Smart Cities / state DOT) for O-D or travel-demand data that reveal real Indian price points, versus the US anchors used here?
- Can the destination-intent label be packaged as a genuinely differentiated product (labeled O-D matrices, modal-intent splits) that StreetLight/Replica-style inference cannot replicate, to justify a premium rather than competing on panel volume?
