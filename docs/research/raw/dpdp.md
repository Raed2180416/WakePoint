# India DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 — compliance for GeoWake, a legal aggregate mobility-data product (Android transit wake-alarm)

## Summary
CRITICAL STATUS CHANGE since the task framing: the "2025 Draft Rules" are no longer draft. The final Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified in the Gazette (G.S.R. 846(E)) on 13 November 2025, operationalising the DPDP Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023). Commencement is phased: Board/definitions provisions are already live; Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) begins 13 Nov 2026; and the core operational obligations for Data Fiduciaries — notice (Rule 3), consent/security/retention, Significant Data Fiduciary duties (Rules 5-16) and Data Principal rights — bind from 13 May 2027. For GeoWake the load-bearing facts are: (1) consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action, purpose-limited and data-minimised (s.6), so monetising aggregate mobility data needs a SEPARATE, opt-in, unbundled commercial-purpose consent that is NOT a precondition of the alarm working; (2) the Act regulates only "personal data" = data about an identifiable individual (s.2(t)) and does NOT define "anonymisation," so genuinely anonymised/aggregate data sits outside the Act — but the bar is high and mobility trajectories are notoriously re-identifiable, so on-device-only aggregation is the safest architecture; (3) penalties reach ₹250 crore for a security-safeguards failure and can stack; (4) Significant Data Fiduciary status is NOT automatic — it applies only if the Central Government notifies you — but if notified you owe an India-based DPO, annual DPIA + independent audit, algorithmic due diligence and possible data-localisation. Children are anyone under 18, requiring verifiable parental consent and a ban on behavioural tracking/targeted ads, so GeoWake should either build verifiable parental consent or gate the service to 18+.

## Key facts
- **The DPDP Rules 2025 are FINAL, not draft: notified in the Gazette of India as G.S.R. 846(E) on 13 November 2025, fully operationalising the DPDP Act 2023. This is the single biggest change vs. the task's 'Draft Rules' framing and happened within the last ~8 months.** — `Gazette G.S.R. 846(E), notified 13 Nov 2025 (published 14 Nov 2025)` [high] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/transforming-data-privacy-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025
- **Commencement is phased. Rules 1,2 and 17-21 (definitions, Data Protection Board) commenced 13 Nov 2025; Rule 4 (Consent Manager registration/obligations) commences 13 Nov 2026; Rules 3, 5-16 and 22-23 (notice, consent, security safeguards, retention, breach reporting, SDF duties, Data Principal rights) commence 13 May 2027. GeoWake's core obligations bind from 13 May 2027, but should be built now.** — `13 Nov 2025 / 13 Nov 2026 / 13 May 2027` [high] (as_of 2026-07-18) https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/
- **Consent standard (Section 6(1)): consent must be 'free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action' and 'limited to such personal data as is necessary for such specified purpose' — i.e. purpose limitation + data minimisation are baked into the consent definition itself.** — `s.6(1) DPDP Act 2023` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpact2023.com/chapter-2
- **Separate commercial-purpose consent: because consent must be 'specific' and 'unconditional' and limited to data 'necessary for such specified purpose', selling/using aggregate mobility data is a DISTINCT purpose from delivering the wake-alarm. It requires its own opt-in consent, must not be bundled with the alarm consent, and cannot be made a precondition of the core service.** — `s.6(1) — 'specific', 'unconditional', 'necessary for such specified purpose'` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpact2023.com/chapter-2
- **Right to withdraw consent (s.6(4)-(6)): a Data Principal may withdraw 'at any time', with 'the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given'; on withdrawal the Fiduciary must cease processing 'within a reasonable time'. Prior processing stays lawful and consequences of withdrawal are borne by the Data Principal.** — `s.6(4)-(6)` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpact2023.com/chapter-2
- **The Act regulates only 'personal data', defined (s.2(t)) as 'any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data'. Identifiability is the trigger, mirroring GDPR.** — `s.2(t)` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section2.html
- **The DPDP Act does NOT define 'anonymisation' or 'anonymised data' (the term does not appear in the s.2 definitions and was dropped from earlier bills). Genuinely anonymised/aggregate data — where no individual is identifiable — therefore falls OUTSIDE the Act, but there is no statutory safe-harbour or defined standard, so the burden is on GeoWake to prove non-identifiability.** — `'anonymised'/'anonymisation' absent from s.2` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section2.html
- **Anonymisation bar is high and mobility-specific: because location trajectories are near-unique and highly re-identifiable, aggregation must defeat singling-out, linkability and inference (EDPB-style). If re-identification remains 'reasonably possible' via data-matching, the data is still personal data and in-scope. Pseudonymised data stays in-scope.** — `Reasonable-risk-of-identification test; EDPB-style criteria advised` [medium] (as_of 2026-07-18) https://amlegals.com/edpbs-new-anonymisation-guidelines-what-they-mean-for-indian-businesses-under-the-dpdp-act/
- **General Data Fiduciary obligations (s.8): accountable regardless of processor; processors only under valid contract (s.8(2)); ensure accuracy where data drives decisions/disclosure (s.8(3)); implement 'reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach' (s.8(5)); notify Board and affected principals of breaches (s.8(6)); ERASE data on consent withdrawal or once purpose is served (s.8(7)); publish DPO/contact (s.8(9)); provide grievance redressal (s.8(10)).** — `s.8(1)-(10)` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpact2023.com/chapter-2
- **Personal data breach must be reported to the Data Protection Board (and affected Data Principals) — the Rules prescribe intimation to the Board with full particulars within 72 hours of becoming aware (Rule 7), plus without-delay notice to affected Data Principals.** — `72 hours to the Board (Rule 7)` [high] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/transforming-data-privacy-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) status is NOT automatic — s.2(z)/s.10 make it a class 'as may be notified by the Central Government', based on volume/sensitivity of data, risk to rights, sovereignty/integrity of India, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State and public order. A niche transit-alarm is unlikely to be notified unless it reaches large scale or sensitive-data volume.** — `s.10(1); designation by Government notification` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://fpf.org/blog/the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-of-india-explained/
- **If notified as an SDF, additional duties apply (s.10(2) + Rule 12): appoint a Data Protection Officer BASED IN INDIA who reports to the Board/senior management; appoint an independent data auditor; conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) and independent audit at least once a year; perform algorithmic 'due diligence' to ensure processing does not risk Data Principals' rights; and comply with any Central Government restriction requiring certain personal/traffic data to be processed only within India (localisation).** — `s.10(2), Rule 12 (incl. Rule 13(4) traffic-data localisation)` [high] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/
- **DPO nuance: only SDFs must appoint an India-based DPO (s.10(2)(a)). A non-SDF Data Fiduciary like GeoWake must still publish the business contact of a DPO 'or of a person able to answer questions' about processing (s.8(9)) and provide grievance redressal (s.8(10)) — but the formal India-resident DPO title is an SDF-only trigger.** — `DPO India-based = SDF only (s.10(2)(a)); contact-point = all fiduciaries (s.8(9))` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://fpf.org/blog/the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-of-india-explained/
- **Penalty Schedule (verbatim): (1) failure of reasonable security safeguards, s.8(5) — 'may extend to two hundred and fifty crore rupees' (₹250 cr); (2) failure to give breach notice, s.8(6) — up to ₹200 cr; (3) breach of children obligations, s.9 — up to ₹200 cr; (4) breach of SDF obligations, s.10 — up to ₹150 cr; (5) breach of Data Principal duties, s.15 — up to ₹10,000; (6) breach of a voluntary undertaking, s.32 — up to the applicable amount; (7) any other provision — up to ₹50 crore.** — `₹250cr / ₹200cr / ₹200cr / ₹150cr / ₹10,000 / applicable / ₹50cr` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpa.com/theschedule.html
- **Penalties are per-contravention and can STACK: a single breach event can trigger the security-safeguards entry (₹250 cr) plus the notification-failure entry (₹200 cr) plus the residual entry (₹50 cr) simultaneously. Section 33(2) sets the factors (nature, gravity, duration, repetition, gain/loss, mitigation, proportionality) the Board weighs within each ceiling.** — `Cumulative exposure; s.33(2) factors` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpa.com/theschedule.html
- **Right to correction and erasure (s.12): a Data Principal may request erasure of her personal data and the Fiduciary must erase unless retention is required for a specified purpose or by law. This is distinct from, and additional to, the s.8(7) duty to erase on withdrawal/purpose-completion.** — `s.12 (erasure right) + s.8(7) (erase on withdrawal/purpose end)` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpact2023.com/chapter-3
- **The fixed 3-year auto-erasure rule does NOT apply to GeoWake. Rule 8's Third Schedule mandatory 3-year retention-then-erasure applies only to named large classes: e-commerce entities with 2 crore+ registered users, online gaming intermediaries with 50 lakh+ users, and social media intermediaries with 2 crore+ users. A transit-alarm is not in these classes, so it follows the GENERAL principle: erase once the purpose is served or consent withdrawn (s.8(7)).** — `Third Schedule: e-commerce ≥2 cr users, gaming ≥50 lakh, social media ≥2 cr; 3 yrs; 48-hr pre-erasure notice` [high] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://www.seclore.com/fundamentals/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-guide/
- **Children: a 'child' is 'an individual who has not completed the age of eighteen years' (s.2(d)/(f)). Fiduciaries must obtain verifiable consent of a parent/lawful guardian before processing a child's data (s.9(1)); must not undertake processing likely to cause detrimental effect (s.9(2)); and are BARRED from tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)). Rule 10 requires verifying the parent's identity and age.** — `child = under 18 (s.2); verifiable parental consent (s.9(1), Rule 10); no tracking/targeted ads (s.9(3))` [high] (as_of 2023-08-11) https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section2.html
- **Rule 3 notice: the consent notice must be standalone ('understandable independently of any other information') and give a fair account enabling specific, informed consent — an itemised description of the personal data collected, the specified purpose with an itemised description of goods/services/uses enabled, and the communication link/means to withdraw consent, exercise rights, and complain to the Board.** — `Rule 3, DPDP Rules 2025` [high] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://www.dpdpa.in/dpdpa_rules_2025/Rule_3.htm
- **Consent Manager (optional, not mandatory) is a Board-registered, India-incorporated intermediary through which Data Principals can give/manage/withdraw consent; registration (Rule 4, effective 13 Nov 2026) requires a minimum net worth of ₹2 crore (inflation-adjusted). GeoWake does not need to be one, but may later integrate with registered Consent Managers.** — `Rule 4; net worth ≥ ₹2 crore; live 13 Nov 2026` [medium] (as_of 2025-11-13) https://www.dpdpa.in/dpdpa_rules_2025/Rule_4.htm

## Caveats
- The Act's substantive obligations (notice, consent, security, retention, rights, SDF duties) are enforceable only from 13 May 2027 under the phased Rules commencement — but building for them now is the correct posture, and Board-related provisions are already live. Enforcement dates, not obligations, are what is deferred.
- There is no statutory definition or safe-harbour for 'anonymisation' in the DPDP Act, and MeitY has not issued anonymisation standards. Treating aggregate mobility data as out-of-scope is a defensible-but-unadjudicated legal position that rests entirely on GeoWake proving genuine non-identifiability. Location trajectories are among the hardest data types to anonymise; a weak aggregation pipeline would pull the data back in-scope with ₹250 cr-tier exposure on a breach.
- This is general regulatory research, not legal advice. Before monetising mobility data, GeoWake should obtain an Indian data-protection lawyer's sign-off on (a) the anonymisation methodology and re-identification risk assessment, and (b) the consent architecture, ideally documented as a formal DPIA even if not an SDF.
- Some Rule-level details (Consent Manager net worth, exact Third Schedule thresholds, Rule 12/13 localisation scope) were confirmed via reputable legal-firm analyses and search-surfaced primary-rule pages rather than a direct fetch of the MeitY Gazette PDF, which returned HTTP 403 this session. The primary numbers are corroborated across EY, S.S. Rana and dpdpa.in, but verify against the official Gazette G.S.R. 846(E) before relying on them contractually.
- The DPDP Act deliberately has NO separate 'sensitive personal data' category (unlike GDPR or the older Indian bills). Location data is not specially classified — but it is fully personal data whenever an individual is identifiable, so the absence of a sensitive-data tier does not lower GeoWake's obligations for raw location.

## Open questions
- What exact aggregation/anonymisation technique will GeoWake use on trajectories (spatial/temporal binning, k-anonymity threshold, differential privacy epsilon, minimum cohort size), and can it be shown to defeat singling-out, linkability and inference for India's dense-transit-corridor data? This determines whether the commercial dataset is in- or out-of-scope.
- Does the on-device pipeline guarantee that raw per-user trajectories NEVER leave the handset (only pre-aggregated/noised outputs transmitted)? If any raw location touches a server, full Data Fiduciary obligations attach to that flow regardless of downstream aggregation.
- Will GeoWake gate the app to 18+ (age self-declaration + no child-directed design) or build a verifiable-parental-consent flow? Given the s.9(3) ban on behavioural tracking of children and a location app's inherent tracking, an 18+ gate is materially simpler to defend.
- Does GeoWake's raw-location retention have a concrete deletion trigger tied to purpose-completion (alarm fired) and to consent withdrawal, with an auditable erasure log, satisfying s.8(7)/s.12 even though the fixed 3-year Third Schedule rule does not apply to it?
- Will the app publish a named grievance-contact/DPO and an in-app withdrawal control that is as easy as the original opt-in (s.6(4)), including a separate, independently revocable toggle for the commercial-data purpose?
- Is there any realistic path to being NOTIFIED as a Significant Data Fiduciary at scale (large user base + granular location volume)? If so, pre-provision an India-based DPO, annual DPIA + independent audit, and algorithmic due-diligence process now.
