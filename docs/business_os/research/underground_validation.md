
# Underground Positioning Validation — Public Data & Methods (Research Report)
Compiled 2026-07-24 for GeoWake. Freshness notes reflect when I found/verified each claim, not necessarily the resource's true last-update date.

## 1. Sussex-Huawei Locomotion (SHL) Dataset

**VERIFIED** (shl-dataset.org, ieee-dataport.org, sussex.ac.uk):
- Contains 8 transportation modes including **Train** and **Subway (Tube)**, collected in southeast England/London by 3 participants each carrying 4 phones (hips, bag, torso, hand) over 7 months.
- Full dataset: ~950 GB, 2,812 labelled hours, 17,562 km travelled.
- Sensors: accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer, **ambient pressure (barometer)**, plus derived orientation quaternion/gravity/linear-acceleration, ~100 Hz. GPS also logged.
- **Access**: full dataset requires an IEEE DataPort subscription. A free **preview** (3 ZIPs, 2.7/2.33/2.12 GB, 59 annotated hours) plus MATLAB loader scripts is available without subscription.
- License file exists but exact terms weren't retrievable from the pages I could fetch (shl-dataset.org itself returned a connection error; details came from the IEEE DataPort mirror).

**ESTIMATE / gap**: I could not confirm from primary sources whether barometer data is intact (vs. dropped) specifically during the Subway/Tube segments — secondary sources note "GPS and barometer have missing segments due to real-world collection conditions" generically, not tunnel-specific. Given London Underground genuinely blocks GPS, the Subway class is exactly the GPS-denied regime GeoWake needs, but you'd need to download the preview and inspect it directly to confirm barometer continuity underground — I could not verify this without downloading actual data files, which is out of scope for this research pass.

**Relevance to GeoWake**: SHL is the best public academic dataset with labeled Subway segments + IMU + barometer, but it's London Underground (deep tube, different tunnel/curvature profile than Indian metros) and only 3 subjects/1 city — useful for validating the EKF's *general* GPS-denied fusion logic (IMU+baro drift correction, mode classification) but not for validating against Indian metro-specific geometry or dwell patterns.

## 2. Other academic IMU/transit datasets

**VERIFIED** (arXiv, ACM, IEEE, Springer):
- **SubwayPS / "Going Deeper Underground"** (Stockx, Hecht, Schöning) — accelerometer+gyroscope-only positioning tested across 4 real subway systems (Brussels, Cologne, London, Minneapolis); reports 85.8% station-detection accuracy, no GPS/WiFi needed. [arxiv.org/abs/1904.01675](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01675)
- **SubwayAPPS** (same/related authors) — barometer-based positioning underground, evaluated against depth profiles of five major world metro systems. [SpringerLink 10.1007/978-3-319-47289-8_4](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-47289-8_4)
- **MLoc — "Crowdsourced Smartphone Sensing for Localization in Metro Trains"** (Haibo Ye et al.) — accelerometer+magnetometer+barometer pattern-map approach, WiFi-free, evaluated on 3 real metro lines / 55 stations, 93% accuracy over 3 stations / 98% over 5 stations. [arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531)
- **SubTrack** (Rutgers) — short-time-energy (STE) based train stop/start detection for real-time subway-riding tracking. [eceweb1.rutgers.edu PDF](http://eceweb1.rutgers.edu/~daisylab/papers/SubTrack-%20Enabling%20Real-time%20Tracking%20of%20Subway%20Riding%20on%20Mobile%20Devices.pdf)
- **"Detecting Arrivals and Departure of Subway Train Using Linear Accelerometer"** — MobiSys'19 poster, arrival/departure detection via linear accelerometer signature. [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3307334.3328635)
- **"Realtime Tracking of Passengers on the London Underground Transport by Matching Smartphone Accelerometer Footprints"** — MDPI Sensors 2019, matches live accelerometer traces against pre-recorded line "footprints." [doi.org/10.3390/s19194184](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19194184)
- **"Learning to Detect the Subway Station Arrival for Mobile Users"** — uses variance peaks in X/Y-axis acceleration plus GSM signal at arrival. [staff.ustc.edu.cn PDF](http://staff.ustc.edu.cn/~cheneh/paper_pdf/2013/KuifeiYu-IDEAL13.pdf)

**Important caveat**: None of these papers appear to publish their raw sensor-trace datasets openly alongside the paper (based on what I could find) — they report methods/results, not reusable public data dumps the way SHL does. If GeoWake wants raw traces, SHL (or self-collected rides) remains the only readily downloadable option; these papers are more valuable as **method blueprints** (stop-detection algorithms, barometer floor-pairing) to replicate on your own recorded rides than as data sources.

**Also found but general-purpose (not transit-specific)**: RIDI dataset (pedestrian IMU, 4 phone positions, 200Hz), SIMD dataset (walking trajectories, 4 cities), and various pedestrian inertial-odometry datasets — useful for general IMU-dead-reckoning model validation but not metro-specific.

## 3. OpenStreetMap rail geometry quality for Indian metros

**VERIFIED (Overpass API query I ran directly against overpass-api.de)**:
- I queried OSM relations tagged `route=subway` matching "Namma Metro / Purple Line / Green Line" and got 51 relations back covering global systems (including Delhi Metro, Namma Metro, Chennai Metro). **None of the 51 relations carried `tunnel`, `layer`, or `location=underground` tags** — those relations are route-level metadata (line name, operator, ref, termini) and do not themselves encode underground/elevated geometry.
- The **OSM Wiki "Metro Mapping" proposal page** confirms tagging convention: underground platforms/ways should get `layer=-N` (N≥3, city-dependent) plus `tunnel=yes` on ways, and `level=-M` where known — but this is a *proposal/convention* page, not a completeness audit. [wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Metro_Mapping](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Metro_Mapping)
- On the discussion page, the proposal's author (Zverik) says he reviewed "more than 150" cities while drafting the proposal, but **no quantitative completeness statistic** ("X% of networks have tunnels mapped") could be verified from primary sources — an earlier AI-summarized search result claimed "very few of 150+ networks have tunnels mapped" but I could not confirm that exact figure in the primary wiki text itself; treat it as **unverified/likely-directionally-true but not a citable number**.

**Practical implication for GeoWake**: tunnel/underground geometry is NOT reliably present on OSM route relations — it lives (if present at all) on individual `way` elements as `tunnel=yes`+`layer=-N`, and per your own system-map, GeoWake's existing rail-geometry skill (`wakepoint-rail-geometry`) already stitches ways into a polyline via Overpass. To determine underground extent per Indian metro line, you'd need to query **ways** (not just the route relation) and filter on `tunnel=yes`/`layer<0`, then check what fraction of the line's arc-length falls in tunnel — this is a per-line audit you can automate with the existing Overpass tooling, not something a single relation-level query answers. I did not run that deeper way-level audit for each Indian metro line in this pass (would need a dedicated Overpass query per line); recommend doing this as a follow-up before relying on OSM tunnel extent for Indian metros specifically.

- Namma Metro system facts (VERIFIED, Wikipedia): 3 operational lines, 83 stations, 96.1 km, of which ~74 stations elevated, 8 underground, 1 at-grade; underground stretch through the historic city centre (Purple Line, Phase 1) required TBM boring; a further ~970m underground stretch (Sampige Rd–Majestic) on the Green Line. Pink Line (u/c) and Blue Line/Airport Line (expected 2026-27) add more mixed elevated/underground/at-grade sections.
- Delhi Metro (VERIFIED, Wikipedia): 374.47 km total; per-line character stated (Yellow Line central section underground, Violet Line "26km overhead + rest underground," Blue Line "mainly elevated, partly underground") but **no aggregate underground/elevated percentage for the whole network was available** from what I fetched.

## 5. Barometer-based floor/tunnel detection research

**VERIFIED** (PMC/NCBI, Tandfonline, ResearchGate):
- Core technique family: relative-pressure-change floor/level detection, several named variants — "Pressure-Pair-Based Floor Localization" / "FloorPair" (near-100% floor accuracy by pairing relative pressure readings between two floors to cancel drift), "MBFP" (multiple reference barometers, doesn't require known building heights). [PMC6720727](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6720727/), [PMC4431287](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4431287/)
- SubwayAPPS specifically validated barometer-based underground positioning by comparing depth profiles across **5 major world metro systems**, showing feasibility of pressure-altimetry positioning underground generally (not India-specific). [Springer 10.1007/978-3-319-47289-8_4](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-47289-8_4)
- General principle applicable to GeoWake: barometric altitude changes are a strong, low-noise "went underground / changed level" discriminator even without absolute calibration, if you use *relative* pressure delta (surface-reference before entering, then drop) rather than absolute-altitude MSL conversion — this matches what fixed-reference "pressure-pair" methods do.

**Not found**: no India-specific barometer/tunnel-depth calibration study for DMRC/BMRCL/etc. — you'd be extrapolating general underground-barometry literature to Indian metros without local validation data.

## 6. WiFi/BLE beacon datasets in metros

**VERIFIED but sparse**: no openly-downloadable public WiFi/BLE fingerprint *dataset* specifically for a metro/subway system was found. What exists is architecture/methodology papers (hybrid WiFi-for-rough-positioning + BLE-for-fine-positioning in "hostile" transit environments — reported accuracy ranges: WiFi 3.94–4.82m / BLE 1.47–2.15m) describing deployments (platforms, near escalators, exits) rather than releasing raw fingerprint data. [NCBI PMC5492348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5492348/)

**Implication**: WiFi/BLE is not a viable near-zero-budget validation path for GeoWake — there's no ready-made public dataset to test against, and building your own beacon infrastructure or fingerprint collection contradicts the "near-zero budget, solo dev" constraint. De-prioritize this relative to IMU+barometer+OSM-geometry approaches.

## 7. Subway stop-detection from accelerometer cadence (station-stop signatures)

**VERIFIED**, converging finding across multiple independent papers — this is a well-established technique:
- Trains decelerating into a station produce a **deceleration "crest"** on the accelerometer, dwell is a flat/quiet segment, then an **acceleration "trough"** on departure — described explicitly in the MLoc paper. [arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531)
- Short-time-energy (STE) thresholding on accelerometer magnitude is used by SubTrack to detect stop/start transitions in real time. [Rutgers PDF](http://eceweb1.rutgers.edu/~daisylab/papers/SubTrack-%20Enabling%20Real-time%20Tracking%20of%20Subway%20Riding%20on%20Mobile%20Devices.pdf)
- Variance-peak detection on X/Y-axis acceleration at arrival, sometimes combined with GSM signal strength as a secondary confirmatory signal. [USTC PDF](http://staff.ustc.edu.cn/~cheneh/paper_pdf/2013/KuifeiYu-IDEAL13.pdf)
- A magnetic-sensor variant also exists for departure detection (different modality, same station-cadence idea). [ACM 3271614](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3271553.3271614)
- Reported accuracies across this literature cluster in the 85–98% range for arrival/station-count detection depending on method and window (higher when counting cumulative stations traveled rather than single-station precision).

**Relevance**: this is directly reusable — GeoWake's EKF likely already has some dwell/stop detection; this literature gives several alternate/complementary signal-processing recipes (STE thresholding, crest-trough matching, variance-peak) that could serve as either (a) a second opinion / sanity check inside the EKF's measurement model, or (b) ground-truth station-boundary labeling when post-processing your own recorded test rides (see plan in §8).

## 8. Concrete plan to generate realistic underground test scenarios from public data

Given the fragmented public-data landscape above, no single dataset gives India-specific ground-truth-labeled underground IMU traces. Recommended layered plan, cheapest/fastest first:

1. **Self-collect labeled reference rides (highest value, near-zero cost).** Ride Namma Metro Purple Line through the Majestic underground stretch (or any Indian metro's underground segment) with a phone logging raw IMU+barometer+GPS at max rate, tapping a "station arrival/departure" button at each stop as manual ground truth. This gives you India-specific, geometry-matched ground truth that no public dataset can — and it's basically free (cost of a metro ticket + your time). Cross-reference station timestamps against the official/community GTFS `stop_times.txt` (Delhi/Hyderabad have official GTFS; for Bengaluru use your existing OSM-derived station positions) to validate your manually-tapped labels.
2. **Use OSM way-level geometry (not the route relation) to build a ground-truth polyline including tunnel extent.** Query Overpass for the line's member `way`s (not just the `route=subway` relation, which — per §3 — carries no tunnel/layer tags), filter for `tunnel=yes`/`layer<0`, and mark those arc-length spans as the "GPS-denied window" your EKF should be tested against. This reuses your existing `wakepoint-rail-geometry` skill's stitching/arc-length/curvature pipeline — just add a tunnel-extent filter pass.
3. **Synthetic GPS-denial injection on real above-ground GTFS+GPS data.** Take a normal above-ground ride (or existing recorded GeoWake test ride) with good GPS, and artificially blank the GPS channel over an interval matching a real tunnel's length/duration (from step 2), then run the EKF in dead-reckoning-only mode across that synthetic blackout and compare its position estimate at "re-acquisition" against the real GPS fix that was blanked. This is the standard "known-truth, artificial denial" validation pattern used in the GNSS-denied-navigation literature I found (synthetic trajectory + noisy IMU + RMSE-vs-truth scoring) — you have real truth because you didn't actually lose the signal, you just hid it from the filter.
4. **Borrow SHL's Subway class as an out-of-domain but real-world GPS-denied stress test.** Even though it's London (different rolling stock, curvature, dwell pattern), the SHL preview (free, no subscription needed, 59 hours) gives real accelerometer+barometer+gyro traces recorded on an actual deep-tube subway with genuinely absent GPS — useful as a "does the EKF stay stable / not diverge catastrophically" smoke test on an independent real-world dataset, separate from your own India-specific rides. Don't use it to tune parameters (wrong geometry), only to catch gross failures (unbounded drift, NaN, filter divergence) your own limited self-collected rides might not exercise.
5. **Apply the accelerometer stop-detection literature (§7) as an independent cross-check.** Implement one of the published lightweight methods (e.g., STE thresholding or crest-trough matching) as a *second, independent* station-boundary detector running in parallel with your EKF's own dwell logic on your self-collected rides. Where the two disagree, that's a labeling/algorithm bug worth investigating — this substitutes for not having independently-verified ground truth from a public dataset.
6. **Barometer relative-pressure calibration per line, from your own rides.** Since no Indian-metro-specific barometer/depth study exists (§5), use your self-collected rides (step 1) to build your own "pressure-pair" reference table (surface pressure at each station entrance vs. platform-level pressure) per line/city, following the FloorPair/MBFP relative-pressure-pairing approach rather than trying to convert to absolute MSL altitude (which drifts with weather).

This plan requires no paid data, no beacon infrastructure, and turns your own limited number of real underground rides into the primary ground-truth source, while using public OSM geometry, public GTFS (where available), and the SHL preview as supporting/cross-checking inputs rather than primary training/validation data.
