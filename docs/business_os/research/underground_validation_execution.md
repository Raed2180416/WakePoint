**Assertions:** Two layers of "never-late" assertion currently exist, both driven by the REAL production `AlarmEvaluator.evaluateCoinciding` (lib/services/alarm_evaluator.dart:50), not a harness-side reimplementation:

The "never-late cone" itself: `ReachabilityTracker.boundNow()` (reachability.dart:937) computes `ReachabilityBound.sMaxMeters` — the physics worst-case forward progress the train could have made since the last trusted anchor, using `Reachability.bound()` (line 543) with the V_LINE ceiling from `VLineTable.forLine()` (line 133, defaults: 28 m/s / 100km/h generic, up to 56 m/s / 200km/h absolute ceiling by keyword/vehicle-type match) and, if `dynamicLeversEnabled` (default false — INERT), a fastest-feasible-train profile (accel/brake/curve caps). This bound feeds `AlarmEvaluator.evaluateCoinciding(reachableProgressBoundMeters: reachBound)` at harness line 647, which internally computes `Reachability.effectiveProgress()` (reachability.dart:863) = `max(statistical_dead_reckoned_progress + sigma_cushion, reach_bound)` — firing when EITHER the EKF's confident estimate OR the physics upper bound passes a stop. THIS max() is the "cone": the harness/production never trusts the EKF alone to prove lateness — the reach bound is a monotone floor under the fire decision. `reach.onAcceptedFix()` (reachability.dart:921) resets the anchor ONLY on a real accepted fix (never on a blanked/skipped one), so a GPS-denial window naturally widens the cone (bound grows via `V_LINE * elapsed_time_since_anchor`) until the next real fix re-anchors it — this is precisely the mechanism a synthetic-GPS-denial scenario would exercise and score.

NOT currently asserted: there is no PER-WINDOW hard gate that checks "does the position estimate at re-acquisition (end of a specific blind window) stay within N meters of ground truth" — `_WindowDiag` (harness line 326) captures `sAtStart/sAtEnd/sBeforeWindow/sAfterWindow/trueAtStart/trueAtEnd` diagnostics for the PHANTOM_INJECT and GAP_INJECT scenarios only (populated via `recordWindow()`, line 691, gated on `cfg.windowDur > 0`), and these are printed but only loosely asserted (e.g. `expect(r.window, isNotNull)` — a smoke check, not a numeric bound). The blind-window notes for `primary`-scenario natural GPS gaps (`blindWindowNotes`, line 878-890) are printed diagnostics only, never gated. This is the gap the requested synthetic-GPS-denial scenario needs to close: a hard, numeric "never-late cone" check keyed to a specific tunnel-blackout window.

## Extracted algorithms (all fetched and read directly, formulas verified against the source PDFs)

### 1. Station-stop detection from accelerometer — three concrete, competing recipes

**(a) SubTrack — STE thresholding** (Liu, Liu, Li, Ma, Chen, Liu, *SubTrack: Enabling Real-time Tracking of Subway Riding on Mobile Devices*, http://eceweb1.rutgers.edu/~daisylab/papers/SubTrack-%20Enabling%20Real-time%20Tracking%20of%20Subway%20Riding%20on%20Mobile%20Devices.pdf)
- Uses **z-axis** accelerometer only (train's minute on-track vibration shows up there), after a **Butterworth high-pass filter, cutoff 50 Hz**, to strip low-frequency (<2 Hz) passenger-posture motion from the true higher-frequency on-the-go vibration.
- Computes **Short-Time Energy (STE)** of that filtered z-axis signal over a sliding window; STE drops sharply and stays low during a stop, high while moving — threshold is "carefully designed" (paper doesn't give the exact numeric γ, only the recipe: filter → STE → threshold → binary moving/stopped).
- Velocity/position: raw velocity = Σ(1/f)·acc(τ) from stop (zero-velocity reset); drift is corrected online using a **linear drift-vs-time model** fit between successive zero-velocity "reference points" (stop periods): `vel_cali(t) = vel(t) − drift_A − (t − t_A)·Δvel`, with `Δvel = (1/N)Σ_k (drift_B^k − drift_A^k)/L_k` averaged over historical stop-pairs (Eq. 1–3).
- Distance: `dis(t) = dis(0) + Σ (1/f)·vel_cali(τ)` (Eq. 4) — reported stop-to-stop distance error **as low as 53 m** average vs. official construction map, on 897–1069 m segments (~5% error).
- Arrival-time model treats the inter-station run as **3 phases** (accel / approx-uniform / decel, Eq. 5), with accel/decel magnitude assumed constant (empirically measured on-device) and phase (`s=1/0/-1`) determined by thresholding STE of the well-aligned moving-direction acceleration.
- Coordinate alignment (quaternion-based gravity + headway alignment, Eq. 6–7) is a prerequisite — phone orientation is arbitrary in-pocket, and this paper's method is the most rigorous of the four for turning raw device-frame accel into train-frame accel.

**(b) MLoc — variance-based crest/trough state machine** (Ye, Gu, Tao, Lu, *Crowdsourced Smartphone Sensing for Localization in Metro Trains*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10531, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10531)
- Per-axis **variation** signal: `VarX_a(t) = X_a(t+Δt) − X_a(t)`, summed as `VarX_all = Σ|VarX_a(t)|` (same for Y, Z) (Eq. 1–3); picks the axis with max mean variation as the combination direction, `Dir(t) = VarK_a(t)/|VarK_a(t)|`, then combines: `Var_All_a(t) = Dir(t) × sqrt(VarX_a(t)² + VarY_a(t)² + VarZ_a(t)²)` (Eq. 6–7). This differencing scheme is explicitly designed to cancel the gravity offset and be orientation-invariant (no coordinate alignment needed, unlike SubTrack).
- **Accel-only state machine** (paper's Fig. 4): `Crest/Trough (variance > 0.4) → Stable (variance < 0.1, held ≥10 s) → StopEvent`.
- **Barometer enhancement state machine** (Fig. 5, used to reject false stops from mid-tunnel deceleration): `Drop/Raise (|pressure_change| > 0.3 hPa) → Stable (variance < 0.15, held ≥10 s) → confirmed door-open/close` — barometer sharply drops ~0.4 hPa on door-open (station A/C leaking to platform) and raises on close; used purely as a confirmatory gate, not primary detection ("we don't use barometer to detect train stop only" because doors can cycle >once or not at all).
- Reported accuracy on 427 real train stops (Nanjing metro-style field study): **accel-only 85% (363/427 correct, 3 wrong, 64 missed); accel+baro fused 96% (410/427 correct, 3 wrong, 17 missed)**. This is the single most citable "accel-only vs accel+baro" comparative number in the literature — directly usable to size the expected improvement from adding baro to GeoWake's EKF.
- Downstream: DTW-based pattern matching (Eq. 9–12) fingerprints each inter-station tunnel by its magnetometer+barometer variance trace, for crowdsourced tunnel identification — 93%/98% accuracy at 3/5-station travel. Less directly relevant to GeoWake (no crowdsourcing plan) but the DTW distance formula is reusable as a trace-similarity metric for validating synthetic vs. real IMU traces.

**(c) HRA sliding-window threshold segmentation** (Hua, Shen, Zhong, *We Can Track You If You Take the Metro*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05958, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.05958 — this is the concrete "quiet-dwell" recipe)
- Computes **Horizontal Resultant Acceleration (HRA)** in an ENU frame (device→ENU via orientation-sensor angles α,β,γ, Table 1 gives exact transform formulas for east/north/vertical components).
- **`FindFinalSegmentPoints` algorithm (their Algorithm 1)**: slide a window `W` of length `L_W` (= the minimum time of a brief metro stop) one sample at a time; find sample `X_i` where the count of values below threshold `T1` within `W_{X_i}` exceeds `95%·L_W` (i.e. the window is "quiet" — this is the "quiet-dwell" detector); then take `X_s` (the minimizer of `Mean(W_{X_s})`) as the segment/stop point, skip forward `T_2` (= shortest station-interval length) before searching for the next stop. `T1` is adaptively increased if segment gaps exceed `L_max` (missed stop) to re-search.
- Sampling: accelerometer @ 10 Hz.
- Accuracy: segmentation edit-distance vs. ground truth — **>90% of segmented sequences have edit distance <2** (i.e., near-perfect station-count recovery even with noise); downstream station-interval *classification* accuracy (which specific interval, via ensemble AdaBoost+RandomForest on 24 statistical + 3 peak/trough features per axis) is **~70% for a 4-station ride, ~90% for a 6-station ride** (basic supervised variant on Nanjing metro line 2, 40 real rides/400 segments).

**(d) SubwayPS — cross-platform-tuned magnitude-threshold recipe** (Stockx, Hecht, Schöning, *SubwayPS: Towards Smartphone Positioning in Underground Public Transportation Systems*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01675, PDF fetched directly)
- Acceleration magnitude `a = sqrt(x²+y²+z²)` (gravity removed via Android's virtual linear-accelerometer, no orientation alignment needed — simplest of the four), smoothed by a **rolling average, window n=100 samples @ 50 Hz** (≈2 s).
- State logic with **4 explicit tunable parameters**: `γ` (threshold), `δ_below` (min. consecutive samples below γ to flag "stopped"), `δ_above` (min. consecutive samples above γ to flag "moving," prevents false positives from user handling the phone). **World-wide default values, empirically fit across 4 real subway systems (Brussels/London/Cologne/Minneapolis): γ=0.2 m/s², δ_below=250 samples, δ_above=350 samples, n=100 @ 50Hz** (their Table 1 — also gives London-tuned δ_above=250 and Cologne-tuned δ_above=500, illustrating per-line retuning matters).
- Accuracy with world-wide params: **74.2%** stop-classification; with London-specific retuning: **85.8%** (103/120 correct across 3 field studies, 8/91 stops missed, **zero false positives** in all 3 studies — an important property for a never-late safety net, since a false "stop detected" would corrupt the zero-velocity reset). Study 2 (50 real Cologne trips): SubwayPS tracked 39/50 trips (78%) with perfect per-stop accuracy vs. only 21/50 (42%) for a pure-timetable baseline — concrete evidence that IMU stop-detection cross-checking beats schedule-only ETA.
- Uses **linear time-interpolation** between confirmed stops to give an in-between position estimate, and flags "unscheduled stop" (train halted mid-tunnel, not at a station) when a detected stop doesn't line up with the timetable — directly analogous to GeoWake's need to distinguish "at a station" dwell from "stuck in tunnel" for the never-late cone.

### 2. Barometer relative-pressure tunnel/floor detection

**FloorPair** (pressure-pair-based floor localization, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6720727/, Sensors 2019)
- Core insight: absolute pressure is unusable (weather drift, per-device offset); use **relative pressure pairs between two known reference points** so drift cancels: `FP(f_pivot, f_probe) = P(f_probe) − P(f_pivot)`, pairs formed only within `T = 5 min` of each other.
- Conversion constant: **h = (P_ref − P_cur) / 0.12** (hPa per meter — note this is the *indoor stairwell* calibration constant they measured, not the standard barometric formula, and is steeper than the ICAO ~0.12 hPa/8.3m rule; worth re-deriving for tunnel depth specifically since metro tunnels are typically 15–25 m deep, giving small (~1.5-3 hPa) but measurable signals).
- Drift-correction via EM: E-step estimates current floor from `P_diff = P_cur − P_ref`; M-step updates the reference pressure `P_ref ← P_cur − Diff_map[f_cur]` only while the user is confirmed stationary (bounded by a pressure-rate check: 0.005–0.025 hPa/s).
- Modern phone barometer noise floor: **σ ≈ 0.004 hPa** (2018-era sensors). Accuracy: near-100%, 0 mean absolute floor error across 12 buildings over months.

**MBFP — Multiple Barometers Floor Positioning** (Xia, Wang, Qiao, Jian, Chang, *Sensors* 2015, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4431287/, https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/15/4/7857)
- Deploys **reference barometers at each floor** (physical infrastructure — not directly portable to a tunnel context without station-platform reference sensors, which GeoWake doesn't have); floor decided by falling within `[pthr_{N+1}, pthr_N]` bracket, comparison done in raw-pressure space (never converts to height), which is what makes it temperature/humidity-robust.
- Explicitly **rejected direct barometric-altitude conversion** — measured max altitude error of 1.04–1.63 m over 20 days from the standard formula `h = −RT₀/(gM)·ln(p/p₀)` due to weather drift, which is why they went to a relative/threshold approach instead.
- Outlier rejection: caps pressure-derived vertical speed at 1 m/s → max plausible pressure jump per window `Δh_max = 2·v_max·t_w`; Grubbs' test for the first window, mean-comparison for later ones. Sampling: 10 Hz; evaluated 1/3/5 s windows.
- Accuracy: **95.65–96.06%** overall; varies sharply by device barometer chip (Mi3/BMP180: 98.6%; Samsung SIII/LPS331AP: 92.1–92.9%) and by carry mode (in-hand 99.4–99.6% vs. in-pocket 95.5%) — a concrete warning that GeoWake's synthetic scenarios should include a device/chip-noise sweep, not assume one barometer SNR.

### 3. Synthesizing a plausible tunnel IMU+baro trace — concrete recipe assembled from the above

The literature gives every component needed to synthesize a scenario without a field ride:
1. **Speed profile**: SubwayPS/SubTrack both empirically confirm real metro inter-station runs decompose into **accel → constant-cruise → decel** (SubTrack Fig. 6, MLoc/SubwayPS corroborate). Synthesize `v(t)` as a trapezoid: choose peak speed (Bengaluru Namma Metro typical ~60-80 km/h elevated / lower under Majestic tunnel curves), accel/decel magnitude (SubTrack observed **≈0.9 m/s² constant** during accel phase — "the train keeps accelerating with constant acceleration around 0.9 m/s² after it departs the station," their Fig. 8/9 caption) — this is a directly reusable synthesis constant.
2. **Accelerometer trace**: forward-axis accel = the trapezoid's derivative (constant ±0.9 m/s² ramps, ~0 cruise); lateral/vertical axes get **track-vibration noise** — SubTrack characterizes this as z-axis STE-detectable "minute vibration" during motion, near-zero variance at rest; MLoc's Var_All formula and SubwayPS's `a=sqrt(x²+y²+z²)` rolling-average-smoothed-to-~0.2 m/s²-at-rest give the target noise floor/threshold to calibrate synthetic noise amplitude against (i.e., inject enough noise that STE/variance detectors calibrated on real thresholds actually fire correctly on the synthetic trace — a built-in self-consistency check).
3. **Dwell**: insert a flat `v=0` segment for the station-stop duration (GeoWake needs real BMRCL dwell times — not found in this literature; would need Bengaluru-specific data, out of scope here) with variance/STE below the detection thresholds (MLoc: variance<0.1 for ≥10s; SubwayPS: δ_below=250 samples≈5s @50Hz below γ=0.2 m/s²).
4. **Barometer trace**: FloorPair/MBFP give the pressure-vs-depth relationship and expected noise floor (σ≈0.004 hPa) to synthesize a plausible tunnel-descent/ascent pressure curve, though **note**: none of the four papers model the metro-specific "piston effect" (train-induced transient pressure waves pushing air through tunnel as it enters/exits, distinct from static depth-vs-pressure) — the SubwayPS paper's related-work section mentions this qualitatively as a real physical effect worth investigating but doesn't quantify it, and no paper found here supplies a formula for it. **This is a literature gap**: synthesizing a realistic tunnel baro trace confidently requires either (a) treating it as static depth-only (defensible baseline, ignores piston transients) or (b) a real Majestic-stretch barometer recording as ground truth, which this literature cannot substitute for.

## Direct implementability verdict

All four station-stop algorithms (STE/SubTrack, variance-state-machine/MLoc, quiet-dwell HRA-window/Hua-et-al, magnitude-threshold/SubwayPS) are precise enough to code directly as independent cross-checks against GeoWake's EKF zero-velocity detection — SubwayPS's is the simplest to port first (single magnitude threshold + 4 tunable ints, published default parameter table, zero-false-positive track record). The barometer recipes (FloorPair, MBFP) are implementable as relative-pressure detectors but solve a *floor* problem, not a *tunnel-depth/piston* problem — using them for GeoWake requires treating "entering a tunnel" as a one-time floor-transition event (defensible) rather than modeling in-tunnel dynamics (not supported by any source found).

Ran the real Namma Metro rides through the EKF replay harness. Added a

### Never-late: PROVEN on real underground data ✓
- NEVER-LATE GATE: **0 late / 0 never-fired** across all rides incl. both real
  Namma recordings.
- Real Majestic ride, ALL GPS withheld (full-tunnel worst case): fires

### Precise underground positioning: NOT achieved — the real finding ✗
Measured along-track error at GPS-denied (underground) stations, natural run
(surface GPS + real blind windows + IMU/ZUPT active):
- **Majestic ride**: max **1330.9 m** drift (Sir M. Visvesvaraya / Central
  College, first deep tunnel). Never-late held (margin 320.7s).
- **Nallur ride**: max **3068.3 m** drift across 7 GPS-denied stations.
  Never-late held (margin 663.2s).

The error **accumulates instead of resetting at each station** — i.e. the
ZUPT + StationAssociation anchoring is NOT reliably re-snapping underground.

### Likely root cause (from code reading — to confirm with diagnostics + data)
A positive-feedback failure in the snap gate (`ekf_orchestrator.dart:513-575`,
`station_association.dart`):
1. StationAssociation's candidate window is `3·σ_s + margin(σ_s)`.
2. During a long tunnel, dead-reckoning drift grows σ_s.
3. A large σ_s widens the window past a full station spacing (~1 km) → MULTIPLE
   candidates → snap rejected (or degraded-nearest picks the wrong/ passed
   station → non-monotonic → rejected by the confidence gate `σ_after ≤ 60m`).
4. No snap → drift keeps growing → window stays wide. It never recovers until
   GPS returns.

So the mechanism works when drift is small (Halasuru: +304m) but fails once
drift crosses ~a station spacing — exactly the deep/long tunnels it's for.

### What this means for the product (honest)
- The **promise ("never late") holds** underground on real data — the rider is
  protected by the physics safety net regardless of EKF drift.
- But the **underground wake is CONSERVATIVE (early)**, not precise, because the
  EKF position can't be trusted mid-long-tunnel. For a destination right after a
  long tunnel, the safety net fires early rather than the EKF firing precisely.
- This is the #1 underground reliability item. Fixing it (robustifying the snap
  gate against accumulated drift — e.g. cadence/step-count-based station
  counting that doesn't depend on absolute σ, per the SubwayPS/MLoc literature)
  needs more real underground data to validate safely — see the data-hunt
  workflow. Do NOT blind-tune it: the never-late guarantee must not regress.
